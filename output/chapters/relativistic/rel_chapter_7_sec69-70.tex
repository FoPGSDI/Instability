\section{Relativistic viscous Couette flow}\label{sec:rel-7-69}

In \S\ref{sec:7-69} we described the classical viscous Couette flow
between two coaxial rotating cylinders.  We now reformulate this
problem in the relativistic framework, using the BDNK
(Bemfica--Disconzi--Noronha--Kovtun) first-order causal
hydrodynamic theory \citep{BemficaDN2018,Kovtun2019,BemficaDN2019,HoultKovtun2020}.

\subsection{Relativistic energy-momentum tensor with BDNK
  viscosity}\label{sec:rel-7-69a}

The energy-momentum tensor for a relativistic dissipative fluid in
the BDNK general-frame formulation is
(cf.\ \textsc{RELATIVISTIC\_CONVENTIONS.md})
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:rel-7-134}
T^{\mu\nu} = \frac{E}{c^{2}}\,u^{\mu}\,u^{\nu}
  + P\,\Delta^{\mu\nu}
  + \Pi^{\mu\nu}
  + \frac{1}{c^{2}}\bigl(Q^{\mu}u^{\nu}+Q^{\nu}u^{\mu}\bigr),
\end{equation}
where $E = \varepsilon + \varepsilon_{1}$ is the frame energy density
(with $\varepsilon_{1}$ the BDNK first-order correction),
$P = p + \Pi_{\mathrm{bulk}}$ is the frame pressure,
$\Pi^{\mu\nu}$ is the traceless anisotropic stress, and
$Q^{\mu}$ is the heat flux four-vector.  The
projection tensor is
$\Delta^{\mu\nu}=g^{\mu\nu}+u^{\mu}u^{\nu}/c^{2}$ and the
four-velocity satisfies $u^{\mu}u_{\mu}=-c^{2}$.

In the BDNK theory the anisotropic stress is given directly by the
\emph{first-order constitutive relation}
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:rel-7-135}
\Pi^{\mu\nu}
  = -2\,\eta_{\mathrm{s}}\,\sigma^{\mu\nu},
\end{equation}
where $\eta_{\mathrm{s}}>0$ is the coefficient of shear viscosity and
$\sigma^{\mu\nu}$ is the relativistic shear tensor
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:rel-7-136}
\sigma^{\mu\nu}
  = \tfrac{1}{2}\bigl(\Delta^{\mu\alpha}\nabla_{\alpha}u^{\nu}
    + \Delta^{\nu\alpha}\nabla_{\alpha}u^{\mu}\bigr)
  - \tfrac{1}{3}\,\Delta^{\mu\nu}\,\nabla_{\alpha}u^{\alpha}.
\end{equation}
Unlike the acausal Eckart or Landau first-order theories, the BDNK
formulation achieves causality and strong hyperbolicity not through
relaxation equations, but through the choice of hydrodynamic frame:
the frame coefficients (entering $\varepsilon_{1}$, $\Pi_{\mathrm{bulk}}$,
and additional terms in $Q^{\mu}$) are constrained by coupled
nonlinear inequalities that ensure all characteristic speeds satisfy
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:rel-7-137}
v_{\mathrm{char}} \le c,
\end{equation}
rigorously and in the full nonlinear theory
\citep{BemficaDN2018,BemficaDNK2023}.
No relaxation times are needed; the only dynamical variables are the
conserved quantities $(\varepsilon, n, u^{\mu})$.

For incompressible flow ($\nabla_{\mu}u^{\mu}=0$) the bulk
viscous contribution vanishes, and we shall henceforth
set $\Pi_{\mathrm{bulk}}=0$ and $Q^{\mu}=0$ (no heat flux).

\subsection{Equilibrium Couette profile: relativistic
  corrections}\label{sec:rel-7-69b}

We work in cylindrical coordinates $(t,r,\varphi,z)$ on a flat
(Minkowski) spacetime.  The equilibrium four-velocity for purely
azimuthal flow with angular velocity $\Omega(r)$ is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:rel-7-139}
u^{\mu} = \gamma\,(c,\,0,\,r\Omega,\,0),
\qquad
\gamma = \Bigl(1-\frac{r^{2}\Omega^{2}}{c^{2}}\Bigr)^{-1/2},
\end{equation}
where $\gamma$ is the Lorentz factor.  The azimuthal velocity is
$V(r)=r\Omega(r)$ and we require $V<c$ everywhere.

For the equilibrium state the shear tensor~\eqref{eq:rel-7-136}
reduces, after projection onto the azimuthal--radial plane, to the
single independent component
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:rel-7-140}
\sigma^{r\varphi}
  = \frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}\,r\,\frac{d\Omega}{dr}.
\end{equation}
In the BDNK framework the constitutive
relation~\eqref{eq:rel-7-135} gives directly
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:rel-7-141}
\Pi^{r\varphi}_{\mathrm{eq}}
  = -2\eta_{\mathrm{s}}\,\sigma^{r\varphi}
  = -\eta_{\mathrm{s}}\,\gamma^{2}\,r\,\frac{d\Omega}{dr}.
\end{equation}

The radial component of the relativistic momentum equation
$\nabla_{\mu}T^{\mu\nu}=0$ yields the centrifugal balance
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:rel-7-142}
\frac{d}{dr}\!\Bigl(\frac{p}{\varepsilon+p}\Bigr)
  = \frac{\gamma^{2}\,V^{2}}{c^{2}\,r}
  + \frac{1}{\varepsilon+p}\,
    \frac{1}{r}\frac{d}{dr}\bigl(r\,\Pi^{r\varphi}_{\mathrm{eq}}\bigr)
    - \text{curvature corrections}.
\end{equation}
The azimuthal component gives the equation for the equilibrium
velocity profile.  Defining the kinematic viscosity
$\nu=\eta_{\mathrm{s}}/w$ with
$w=(\varepsilon+p)/c^{2}$ the relativistic enthalpy density, the
azimuthal equilibrium condition becomes
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:rel-7-143}
\nu\,\gamma^{2}\,\frac{d}{dr}\!\left[
  \gamma^{2}\,\frac{d}{dr}\!\Bigl(
    \frac{\gamma\,V}{r}\Bigr)\,r^{3}
\right] = 0.
\end{equation}
In the non-relativistic limit $\gamma\to 1$ this reduces to
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:rel-7-144}
\nu\,\frac{d}{dr}\!\left(\frac{dV}{dr}+\frac{V}{r}\right) = 0,
\end{equation}
which is precisely equation~\eqref{eq:7-142} of \S\ref{sec:7-69},
confirming the classical Couette solution
$V=Ar+B/r$.

For the relativistic case, equation~\eqref{eq:rel-7-143} admits the
solution
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:rel-7-145}
V(r) = Ar + \frac{B}{r}
  + \frac{A^{3}r^{3}+3A^{2}Br+3AB^{2}/r+B^{3}/r^{3}}{2c^{2}}
  + O(V^{4}/c^{4}),
\end{equation}
so that the angular velocity acquires relativistic corrections:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:rel-7-146}
\Omega(r) = A + \frac{B}{r^{2}}
  + \frac{1}{2c^{2}}\Bigl(A + \frac{B}{r^{2}}\Bigr)^{3}r^{2}
  + O(c^{-4}),
\end{equation}
where $A$ and $B$ are determined by the boundary conditions on the
two cylinders exactly as in the classical case
(equation~\eqref{eq:7-145}), with $O(V^{2}/c^{2})$ corrections.

The corresponding expression for the constants is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:rel-7-147}
A = -\Omega_{1}\eta^{2}\,\frac{1-\mu/\eta^{2}}{1-\eta^{2}}
  \bigl[1+O(R_{1}^{2}\Omega_{1}^{2}/c^{2})\bigr],
\qquad
B = \Omega_{1}\,\frac{R_{1}^{2}(1-\mu)}{1-\eta^{2}}
  \bigl[1+O(R_{1}^{2}\Omega_{1}^{2}/c^{2})\bigr],
\end{equation}
with $\mu=\Omega_{2}/\Omega_{1}$ and $\eta=R_{1}/R_{2}$ retaining
their classical definitions (equation~\eqref{eq:7-146}).

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Remark (causality).}  In the acausal Navier--Stokes
(Eckart/Landau) formulation the viscous equilibrium~\eqref{eq:rel-7-144}
is instantaneously established.  In the BDNK theory, causality is
ensured by the frame-dependent structure of the constitutive relations
rather than by relaxation dynamics.  The stationary
profiles~\eqref{eq:rel-7-145} and~\eqref{eq:rel-7-146} are identical
in both formulations---the difference arises in the perturbation
dynamics and the causal propagation of viscous signals.

\section{The perturbation equations: relativistic
  formulation}\label{sec:rel-7-70}

We now investigate the stability of the relativistic Couette flow
described in \S\ref{sec:rel-7-69}.  The perturbation analysis follows
the structure of the classical treatment in \S\ref{sec:7-70}, with the
viscous sector governed by the BDNK first-order constitutive
relation~\eqref{eq:rel-7-135}.

\subsection{Linearized relativistic equations}\label{sec:rel-7-70a}

Let the perturbed state be characterised by
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:rel-7-148}
u^{\mu} = u^{\mu}_{\mathrm{eq}} + \delta u^{\mu},
\quad
p = p_{\mathrm{eq}} + \delta p,
\quad
\varepsilon = \varepsilon_{\mathrm{eq}} + \delta\varepsilon,
\quad
\Pi^{\mu\nu} = \Pi^{\mu\nu}_{\mathrm{eq}} + \delta\Pi^{\mu\nu},
\end{equation}
where perturbations are taken to be axisymmetric
($\partial_{\varphi}=0$) and of first order.

Writing the perturbation velocity components in the local rest frame
as $u_{r}$, $u_{\varphi}$, $u_{z}$, and the pressure perturbation as
$\varpi=\delta p/w$ (with $w=(\varepsilon+p)/c^{2}$), the linearized
radial, azimuthal, and axial momentum equations become
\begin{align}
\label{eq:rel-7-149}
\gamma^{2}\frac{\partial u_{r}}{\partial t}
  - \frac{2\gamma^{2}V}{r}\,u_{\varphi}
  &= -\frac{\partial\varpi}{\partial r}
  + \frac{1}{w}\,\nabla_{\mu}\delta\Pi^{\mu r}
  + \frac{\gamma^{2}V^{2}}{c^{2}r}\,
    \frac{\delta\varepsilon}{\varepsilon+p}\,,
\\[6pt]
\label{eq:rel-7-150}
\gamma^{2}\frac{\partial u_{\varphi}}{\partial t}
  + \gamma^{2}\!\left(\frac{dV}{dr}+\frac{V}{r}\right)u_{r}
  &= \frac{1}{w}\,\nabla_{\mu}\delta\Pi^{\mu\varphi},
\\[6pt]
\label{eq:rel-7-151}
\frac{\partial u_{z}}{\partial t}
  &= -\frac{\partial\varpi}{\partial z}
  + \frac{1}{w}\,\nabla_{\mu}\delta\Pi^{\mu z}.
\end{align}
The continuity equation (baryon conservation) for axisymmetric
perturbations is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:rel-7-152}
\frac{\partial(\gamma\,u_{r})}{\partial r}
  + \frac{\gamma\,u_{r}}{r}
  + \frac{\partial u_{z}}{\partial z}
  + \frac{\gamma^{3}V}{c^{2}}\,\frac{\partial u_{r}}{\partial t}
  = 0.
\end{equation}
In the non-relativistic limit $\gamma\to 1$, the last term vanishes
and equations~\eqref{eq:rel-7-149}--\eqref{eq:rel-7-152} reduce
exactly to equations~\eqref{eq:7-149}--\eqref{eq:7-153} of
\S\ref{sec:7-70}.

\subsection{BDNK perturbation of the anisotropic
  stress}\label{sec:rel-7-70b}

In the BDNK first-order theory, the viscous stress perturbation
$\delta\Pi^{\mu\nu}$ is given directly by the linearised constitutive
relation
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:rel-7-153}
\delta\Pi^{\mu\nu}
  = -2\eta_{\mathrm{s}}\,\delta\sigma^{\mu\nu},
\end{equation}
where $\delta\sigma^{\mu\nu}$ is the perturbed shear tensor.
Unlike the Israel--Stewart second-order theory, no relaxation equation
is needed; the viscous stress is algebraically determined by the
velocity gradient at each instant.  Causality is nevertheless ensured
by the BDNK frame coefficients, which modify the characteristic
structure of the conservation equations to guarantee strong
hyperbolicity \citep{BemficaDN2018,Kovtun2019}.

The explicit components of $\delta\sigma^{\mu\nu}$ in cylindrical
coordinates for axisymmetric perturbations are
\begin{align}
\label{eq:rel-7-154}
\delta\sigma^{rr} &= \gamma^{2}\,\frac{\partial u_{r}}{\partial r}
  - \tfrac{1}{3}\gamma^{2}\,\nabla_{\mu}\delta u^{\mu},
\\
\delta\sigma^{r\varphi} &= \tfrac{1}{2}\gamma^{2}
  \Bigl(\frac{\partial u_{\varphi}}{\partial r}
  - \frac{u_{\varphi}}{r}
  + \frac{\gamma^{2}V}{c^{2}}\frac{\partial u_{r}}{\partial t}\Bigr),
\\
\delta\sigma^{zz} &= \frac{\partial u_{z}}{\partial z}
  - \tfrac{1}{3}\gamma^{2}\,\nabla_{\mu}\delta u^{\mu}.
\end{align}

\subsection{Normal mode analysis}\label{sec:rel-7-70c}

Following the classical treatment, we decompose the perturbations
into normal modes:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:rel-7-155}
\begin{aligned}
u_{r} &= e^{pt}\,u(r)\cos kz, &\quad
u_{\varphi} &= e^{pt}\,v(r)\sin kz, \\
u_{z} &= e^{pt}\,w(r)\cos kz, &\quad
\varpi &= e^{pt}\,\hat\varpi(r)\cos kz, \\
\delta\Pi^{\mu\nu} &= e^{pt}\,\Pi^{\mu\nu}(r)
  \times\{\cos kz \text{ or } \sin kz\},
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where $k$ is the axial wave number and $p$ may be complex.  In the
BDNK framework the constitutive relation~\eqref{eq:rel-7-153}
gives directly for each mode
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:rel-7-156}
\hat{\Pi}^{\mu\nu}(r)
  = -2\eta_{\mathrm{s}}\,
    \delta\sigma^{\mu\nu}_{\mathrm{mode}}(r),
\end{equation}
so that the viscous term in the momentum equations enters through the
\emph{kinematic viscosity}
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:rel-7-157}
\nu = \frac{\eta_{\mathrm{s}}}{w},
\end{equation}
which is frequency-independent (unlike the Israel--Stewart theory
where an effective viscosity $\nu/(1+\tau_{\pi}p)$ appears).
Causality in the BDNK theory is ensured not by frequency-dependent
damping but by frame-dependent bounds on the transport coefficients
\citep{Kovtun2019,HoultKovtun2020}.  At the marginal state $p=0$,
both BDNK and Israel--Stewart reduce to the same eigenvalue problem
with viscosity~$\nu$.

Substituting~\eqref{eq:rel-7-156} into the linearised momentum
equations and carrying out the same elimination of $w$ and $\varpi$
as in the classical \S\ref{sec:7-70}, we arrive at the pair of
coupled equations (measuring $r$ in units of $R_{2}$, with
$a^{2}=k^{2}R_{2}^{2}$ and
$\sigma=pR_{2}^{2}/\nu$):
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:rel-7-158}
\bigl(DD_{*}-a^{2}\bigr)
\Bigl[\gamma^{2}\bigl(DD_{*}-a^{2}\bigr)
  - \sigma\gamma^{2}\Bigr]u
= -T_{\mathrm{rel}}\,a^{2}
  \Bigl(\frac{1}{r^{2}}-\kappa_{\mathrm{rel}}\Bigr)v,
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:rel-7-159}
\gamma^{2}
  \bigl(DD_{*}-a^{2}\bigr)v
  - \sigma\gamma^{2}v
= \bigl(D_{*}V\bigr)_{\mathrm{rel}}\,u,
\end{equation}
where $D$ and $D_{*}$ retain their classical meanings
(equation~\eqref{eq:7-106}) and we have introduced the
\emph{relativistic Taylor number}
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:rel-7-160}
T_{\mathrm{rel}}
  = -\frac{4A_{\mathrm{rel}}B_{\mathrm{rel}}}{R_{2}^{4}}
  = T_{\mathrm{cl}}
    \times\gamma_{1}^{2}\gamma_{2}^{2}
    \bigl[1+O(V^{2}/c^{2})\bigr],
\end{equation}
with $T_{\mathrm{cl}}$ the classical Taylor number of
equation~\eqref{eq:7-169}, and the relativistic analogue of
$\kappa$:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:rel-7-161}
\kappa_{\mathrm{rel}}
  = -\frac{A_{\mathrm{rel}}\,R_{2}^{4}}{B_{\mathrm{rel}}}
  = \kappa\bigl[1+O(V^{2}/c^{2})\bigr].
\end{equation}
The notation $\gamma_{1}=\gamma(R_{1})$,
$\gamma_{2}=\gamma(R_{2})$ refers to the Lorentz factor evaluated
at each cylinder wall.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Remark.}  The structure of
equations~\eqref{eq:rel-7-158} and~\eqref{eq:rel-7-159} is
identical to the classical system~\eqref{eq:7-167}--\eqref{eq:7-168}
with one modification: factors of $\gamma^{2}$ multiply the inertial
terms, reflecting the relativistic enthalpy.  In the BDNK theory the
viscosity $\nu$ appears without frequency-dependent modification;
causality is instead enforced by the BDNK frame coefficients which
bound the characteristic speeds of the conservation equations.

\subsection{Stability for $\mu>\eta^{2}$: relativistic
  generalisation}\label{sec:rel-7-70d}

We now establish that the Rayleigh criterion $\mu>\eta^{2}$ remains a
\emph{sufficient condition for stability} in the relativistic theory.

The argument follows the classical energy-integral method of
\S\ref{sec:7-70}.  Multiply equation~\eqref{eq:rel-7-158} by
$r\,u^{*}$ and equation~\eqref{eq:rel-7-159} by
$r\,T_{\mathrm{rel}}\,a^{2}\phi_{\mathrm{rel}}(r)\,v^{*}$, where
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:rel-7-162}
\phi_{\mathrm{rel}}(r)
  = (1-\mu)\Bigl(\frac{1}{r^{2}}-\kappa_{\mathrm{rel}}\Bigr)
    \gamma^{2}(r),
\end{equation}
integrate over the gap $\eta\le r\le 1$, and use the boundary
conditions (no-slip on both walls):
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:rel-7-163}
u=v=0, \qquad Du=0
\qquad\text{at } r=1 \text{ and } r=\eta.
\end{equation}
After integration by parts (using the same identities~\eqref{eq:7-172}
and~\eqref{eq:7-173} as in the classical case), one obtains an
equation of the form
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:rel-7-164}
\mathrm{re}(\sigma)\,
  \bigl\{I_{1}^{\mathrm{rel}}
    - \mathscr{T}_{\mathrm{rel}}\,a^{2}\,I_{3}^{\mathrm{rel}}
    + \mathrm{re}(I_{4}^{\mathrm{rel}})\bigr\} = 0,
\end{equation}
where the relativistic integral functionals are
\begin{align}
\label{eq:rel-7-165}
I_{1}^{\mathrm{rel}}
  &= \int_{\eta}^{1}\gamma^{2}(r)
     \Bigl\{r\Bigl|\frac{du}{dr}\Bigr|^{2}
     + \Bigl(\frac{1}{r}+a^{2}r\Bigr)|u|^{2}\Bigr\}\,dr,
\\[4pt]
I_{3}^{\mathrm{rel}}
  &= \int_{\eta}^{1}\phi_{\mathrm{rel}}(r)\,|v|^{2}\,dr,
\\[4pt]
\mathrm{re}(I_{4}^{\mathrm{rel}})
  &= \int_{\eta}^{1}r\,\gamma^{2}(r)\,\phi_{\mathrm{rel}}(r)
     \Bigl|\frac{dv}{dr}-\frac{v}{r}\Bigr|^{2}\,dr.
\end{align}
The factors $\gamma^{2}(r)\ge 1$ are everywhere positive and do not
alter the sign-definiteness of the integrands.  When $\mu>\eta^{2}$
we have $\mathscr{T}_{\mathrm{rel}}<0$ (just as in the classical
case, equation~\eqref{eq:7-189}), so the coefficient of
$\mathrm{re}(\sigma)$ in~\eqref{eq:rel-7-164} is
\emph{positive definite}.  Therefore
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:rel-7-166}
\boxed{\mathrm{re}(\sigma) < 0
  \qquad\text{for } \mu > \eta^{2},}
\end{equation}
and the relativistic viscous Couette flow is stable whenever
Rayleigh's criterion is satisfied.  This is the relativistic
generalisation of equation~\eqref{eq:7-189}.

\subsection{Eigenvalue problem for the relativistic Taylor
  number}\label{sec:rel-7-70e}

The marginal state is defined by $\mathrm{re}(p)=0$.  Following
the classical analysis, we expect the marginal state to be
\emph{stationary} ($p=0$, exchange of stabilities).  In the BDNK
theory there is no frequency-dependent effective viscosity, so the
eigenvalue problem at onset takes the same form as in the classical
case with $\nu$ replacing the Newtonian kinematic viscosity.  In this case
equations~\eqref{eq:rel-7-158}--\eqref{eq:rel-7-159} reduce to the
eigenvalue problem
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:rel-7-167}
\gamma^{2}(DD_{*}-a^{2})^{2}u
  = -T_{\mathrm{rel}}\,a^{2}
    \Bigl(\frac{1}{r^{2}}-\kappa_{\mathrm{rel}}\Bigr)v,
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:rel-7-168}
\gamma^{2}(DD_{*}-a^{2})v = u,
\end{equation}
subject to the boundary conditions~\eqref{eq:rel-7-163}.  This is a
generalised eigenvalue problem for $T_{\mathrm{rel}}$ as a function
of the dimensionless wave number~$a$.  The critical relativistic
Taylor number is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:rel-7-169}
T_{\mathrm{rel,cr}} = \min_{a}\,T_{\mathrm{rel}}(a)
  = T_{\mathrm{cl,cr}}
    \bigl[1+O(V^{2}/c^{2})\bigr],
\end{equation}
where $T_{\mathrm{cl,cr}}$ is the classical critical Taylor number.
The relativistic corrections are of order $V^{2}/c^{2}$ and
increase the critical Taylor number, i.e.\ relativity has a
\emph{stabilising} effect.  This is physically expected: the
enhanced inertia ($\gamma^{2}$ factors) makes it harder to displace
fluid elements against the centrifugal gradient.

\subsection{BDNK causality bounds for oscillatory
  modes}\label{sec:rel-7-70f}

In the BDNK theory the viscosity $\nu$ is frequency-independent,
so for oscillatory modes ($p=i\omega$) the viscous damping rate
takes the standard form
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:rel-7-170}
\mathrm{re}(p)
  = -\nu k^{2} + \cdots,
\end{equation}
which is the same as in the Navier--Stokes theory.  The crucial
difference is that the BDNK frame coefficients ensure that the
\emph{characteristic speeds} of the full system of conservation
equations remain bounded by~$c$.  This is guaranteed by the
BDNK causality conditions---coupled inequalities on
$(\eta_{\mathrm{s}}, \zeta, \kappa, \text{frame coefficients})$---which
replace the Israel--Stewart requirement $\tau_{\pi}>0$
\citep{BemficaDN2018,Kovtun2019}.

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:rel-7-171}
v_{\mathrm{char}} \le c
\qquad\text{(BDNK causality bound)},
\end{equation}
ensuring that perturbation fronts remain subluminal at all
frequencies.  The BDNK theory thus achieves the same causal
behaviour as Israel--Stewart but with a simpler (first-order)
constitutive structure and without introducing additional dynamical
degrees of freedom.

\subsection{Summary of relativistic modifications}\label{sec:rel-7-70g}

The relativistic treatment of viscous Couette flow and its stability
introduces the following modifications to the classical theory of
\S\S\ref{sec:7-69}--\ref{sec:7-70}:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Equilibrium profile:}  The velocity
  $V(r)=Ar+B/r$ receives corrections of order $V^{2}/c^{2}$
  (equation~\eqref{eq:rel-7-145}).
\item \textbf{Viscous constitutive law:}  The Navier--Stokes
  relation is replaced by the BDNK first-order constitutive
  relation $\Pi^{\mu\nu}=-2\eta_{\mathrm{s}}\sigma^{\mu\nu}$,
  with causality ensured by frame-dependent bounds on the
  transport coefficients \citep{BemficaDN2018,Kovtun2019}.
\item \textbf{Perturbation equations:}  The eigenvalue problem
  acquires $\gamma^{2}$ factors from the relativistic enthalpy;
  the viscosity $\nu$ enters without frequency-dependent modification.
\item \textbf{Sufficient stability condition:}  The criterion
  $\mu>\eta^{2}$ remains sufficient for stability, with the proof
  carrying through thanks to the positive-definiteness of
  $\gamma^{2}$.
\item \textbf{Critical Taylor number:}  $T_{\mathrm{rel,cr}}>
  T_{\mathrm{cl,cr}}$; relativity stabilises the flow.
\item \textbf{Causality:}  All characteristic speeds are
  bounded by $v_{\mathrm{char}}\le c$, guaranteed by the BDNK
  causality conditions on the frame coefficients.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Astrophysical application: viscous accretion disks and
  the $\alpha$-parameter}
\label{sec:rel-7-70-astro}

The viscous Couette analysis of this section has an immediate
application to the Shakura--Sunyaev $\alpha$-disk model
\citep{ShakuraSunyaev1973}, which parametrises the turbulent viscosity
as $\nu=\alpha\,c_{s}\,H$ where $c_{s}$ is the sound speed and $H$
the disk scale height.  In the relativistic BDNK framework, the
kinematic viscosity is $\nu_{\mathrm{rel}}=\eta_{\mathrm{s}}c^{2}/
(\varepsilon+p)$ rather than $\eta_{\mathrm{s}}/\rho_{0}$, so the
effective $\alpha$-parameter is modified:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:rel-7-172}
\alpha_{\mathrm{rel}}
= \alpha_{\mathrm{cl}}
  \times\frac{\rho_{0}\,c^{2}}{\varepsilon+p}
= \frac{\alpha_{\mathrm{cl}}}{1+\xi}\,,
\end{equation}
where $\xi=(\varepsilon+p)/(\rho_{0}c^{2})-1$ is the relativistic
correction parameter.  For a radiation-dominated disk ($p\sim\varepsilon/3$)
near a stellar-mass black hole, $\xi\sim 0.3$ and $\alpha_{\mathrm{rel}}$
is reduced by $\sim 23\%$ relative to the classical value.

The effective Reynolds number, conversely, is \emph{enhanced}:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:rel-7-173}
\mathrm{Re}_{\mathrm{rel}}
= \frac{V\,d}{\nu_{\mathrm{rel}}}
= \mathrm{Re}_{\mathrm{cl}}\,(1+\xi),
\end{equation}
reflecting the increased inertia per unit viscous stress.  The critical
Taylor number is raised by $(1+\xi)^{2}$, but since $\mathrm{Re}$
also grows, the net effect is that the onset velocity for instability
is reduced.

Figure~\ref{fig:viscous-disk-alpha} illustrates these relativistic
corrections.  The left panel shows
$\mathrm{Re}_{\mathrm{rel}}/\mathrm{Re}_{\mathrm{cl}}$ as a function
of~$\xi$, with representative astrophysical regimes marked.  The right
panel compares the relativistic enhancement of the critical Taylor
number with the reduction in the effective $\alpha$-viscosity.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.95\textwidth]{fig_viscous_disk_Re_alpha.pdf}
\caption{Left: ratio of relativistic to classical Reynolds number
  versus the correction parameter~$\xi$, with astrophysical regimes
  indicated.  Right: relativistic enhancement of the critical Taylor
  number (solid) and suppression of the Shakura--Sunyaev
  $\alpha$-parameter (dashed) as functions of~$\xi$.}
\label{fig:viscous-disk-alpha}
\end{figure}

\subsubsection{Radial profile of BDNK corrections in an $\alpha$-disk}

The effective BDNK Reynolds number and the modification to the
Shakura--Sunyaev $\alpha$-parameter depend on the local thermodynamic
state of the disk, which varies with radius.  In the standard
$\alpha$-disk model \citep{ShakuraSunyaev1973}, the disk is divided
into three zones:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] \textbf{Radiation-dominated, electron-scattering opacity}
  ($r\lesssim 150\,r_{g}$): here $p_{\mathrm{rad}}\gg p_{\mathrm{gas}}$
  and the relativistic correction parameter
  $\xi=(\varepsilon+p)/(\rho_{0}c^{2})-1$ can reach $\xi\sim 0.1$--$0.5$
  for accretion rates $\dot{M}\sim 0.1\,\dot{M}_{\mathrm{Edd}}$.
\item[(b)] \textbf{Gas-dominated, electron-scattering opacity}
  ($150\,r_{g}\lesssim r\lesssim 5000\,r_{g}$): $\xi\sim 10^{-2}$,
  with the Novikov--Thorne page factor controlling the radial
  scaling \citep{NovikovThorne1973}.
\item[(c)] \textbf{Gas-dominated, free-free opacity}
  ($r\gtrsim 5000\,r_{g}$): $\xi\sim 10^{-3}$ or smaller; BDNK
  corrections become negligible.
\end{enumerate}

Figure~\ref{fig:bdnk-alpha-disk} shows the radial profiles of $\xi(r)$,
$\alpha_{\mathrm{rel}}/\alpha_{\mathrm{cl}}$, and
$\mathrm{Re}_{\mathrm{rel}}/\mathrm{Re}_{\mathrm{cl}}$ for a
$10\,M_{\odot}$ black hole with $\alpha_{\mathrm{cl}}=0.1$ at three
accretion rates.  The key results are:
\begin{itemize}
\item In the radiation-dominated inner zone, the BDNK kinematic
  viscosity $\nu_{\mathrm{rel}}=\eta_{\mathrm{s}}c^{2}/(\varepsilon+p)$
  is reduced by up to $\sim 30\%$ relative to the classical
  $\nu=\eta_{\mathrm{s}}/\rho_{0}$, suppressing the effective
  $\alpha$-parameter.
\item The Reynolds number is correspondingly enhanced by the
  factor $(1+\xi)$, so the effective inertia of the relativistic
  fluid is larger per unit viscous stress.
\item The transition between zones~(a) and~(b) produces a
  characteristic break in $\xi(r)$ at $r\sim 150\,r_{g}$, visible
  as a change of slope in all three panels.
\item For sub-Eddington accretion ($\dot{M}/\dot{M}_{\mathrm{Edd}}
  \lesssim 0.01$), the corrections are $\lesssim 1\%$ everywhere
  and the classical Shakura--Sunyaev prescription is adequate.
\end{itemize}

These results have implications for the dissipative dynamics revealed
by GRMHD simulations of accretion flows.  The BDNK formalism
\citep{MostNoronha2021} provides a first-principles foundation for
incorporating causal viscosity into disk models, replacing the ad hoc
$\alpha$-prescription with transport coefficients constrained by
causality and stability.  The EHT observations of M87
\citep{EHT2019M87V} and the accompanying GRMHD code comparison
\citep{Porth2019} provide an observational context in which these
BDNK corrections could, in principle, be tested.
