\section{Stability of the simplest solution of the equations of
relativistic hydromagnetics}
\label{sec:rel-113}

In the Newtonian theory (\S\ref{sec:113}), the simplest solution of
the ideal MHD equations for an incompressible, inviscid fluid of
infinite electrical conductivity is the \emph{equipartition solution}
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:rel-12-215}
\mathbf{u}_{s} = \pm H_{s}\sqrt{\frac{\mu}{4\pi\rho}},
\qquad
\Pi = \frac{p}{\rho} + \mathfrak{B}
  + \frac{\mu|\mathbf{H}|^{2}}{8\pi\rho} = \text{const},
\end{equation}
whose stability Chandrasekhar established by an energy argument.  In
the relativistic setting, the fluid 4-velocity $u^{\mu}$ and the
magnetic 4-vector $b^{\mu}$ replace $\mathbf{u}$ and $\mathbf{H}$,
and the energy--momentum tensor acquires relativistic enthalpy
$\enthalpy = (\varepsilon + p)/c^{2}$ in its inertial part.  We derive
the relativistic analogue of the equipartition stability theorem and
then work out the explicit kink/sausage modes in a cylindrical column.

\subsection{Covariant ideal MHD and the relativistic equipartition
solution}
\label{sec:rel-113-cov}

We work in Minkowski spacetime with signature $(-,+,+,+)$.  The ideal
MHD condition $F^{\mu\nu}u_{\nu} = 0$ ensures that the electric field
vanishes in the fluid rest frame.  The total energy--momentum tensor is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:rel-113-Tmunu}
T^{\mu\nu} = \bigl(\enthalpy c^{2} + b^{2}\bigr)
  \frac{u^{\mu}u^{\nu}}{c^{2}}
  + \Bigl(p + \frac{b^{2}}{2}\Bigr)g^{\mu\nu}
  - b^{\mu}b^{\nu},
\end{equation}
where $b^{2} = b^{\alpha}b_{\alpha}$ and we have set the permeability
$\mu/(4\pi) \to 1$ in Heaviside--Lorentz units appropriate to the
covariant formulation (the classical Gaussian factors are restored
when taking the Newtonian limit).

The equations of motion $\nabla_{\mu}T^{\mu\nu} = 0$, together with
the ideal induction equation
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:rel-113-induction}
u^{\mu}\nabla_{\mu}b^{\nu} = b^{\mu}\nabla_{\mu}u^{\nu}
  - b^{\nu}\nabla_{\mu}u^{\mu},
\end{equation}
and the constraint $b^{\mu}u_{\mu} = 0$, constitute the relativistic
ideal MHD system.

\begin{relcorrection}
\textbf{Relativistic equipartition solution.}
The classical equipartition condition $\mathbf{u} = \pm\mathbf{H}
\sqrt{\mu/(4\pi\rho)}$ generalises to the relativistic Alfv\'en
condition
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:rel-113-equip}
\delta u^{\mu} = \pm \frac{b^{\mu}}{\sqrt{\enthalpy c^{2} + b^{2}}}\,c,
\end{equation}
where $\delta u^{\mu}$ is the perturbation 3-velocity projected into
the local rest frame.  The denominator
$\sqrt{\enthalpy c^{2} + b^{2}}$ reduces to $\sqrt{4\pi\rho/\mu}$ in
the Newtonian limit ($\enthalpy \to \rho$, $b^{2}/(4\pi) \to
\mu H^{2}/(8\pi)$).  The key relativistic modification is the
replacement of the mass density $\rho$ by the \emph{relativistic
inertia} $\enthalpy + b^{2}/c^{2}$, which includes contributions from
the thermal energy density, the pressure, and the magnetic energy.
\end{relcorrection}

\subsection{Stability of the relativistic equipartition state}
\label{sec:rel-113-stab}

Following Chandrasekhar's strategy, we define the Elsasser-like
variables
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:rel-113-XY}
X^{\mu} = \delta b^{\mu} - \sqrt{\enthalpy c^{2}+b^{2}}\;\delta u^{\mu}/c,
\qquad
Y^{\mu} = \delta b^{\mu} + \sqrt{\enthalpy c^{2}+b^{2}}\;\delta u^{\mu}/c,
\end{equation}
where $\delta b^{\mu}$ and $\delta u^{\mu}$ are linearised
perturbations about the equilibrium.  In the equipartition state the
background satisfies $X^{\mu} = 0$ (or $Y^{\mu} = 0$, depending on
the sign choice), and the linearised perturbation equations for the
symmetric pair $(X^{\mu}, Y^{\mu})$ take the form
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:rel-113-Xeq}
u^{\alpha}_{(0)}\nabla_{\alpha}X^{\mu}
  + Y^{\alpha}\nabla_{\alpha}u^{\mu}_{(0)}
  = -\frac{\Delta^{\mu\nu}}{\enthalpy c^{2}+b^{2}}\,\nabla_{\nu}\delta\Pi_{\mathrm{tot}},
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:rel-113-Yeq}
u^{\alpha}_{(0)}\nabla_{\alpha}Y^{\mu}
  + X^{\alpha}\nabla_{\alpha}u^{\mu}_{(0)}
  = -\frac{\Delta^{\mu\nu}}{\enthalpy c^{2}+b^{2}}\,\nabla_{\nu}\delta\Pi_{\mathrm{tot}},
\end{equation}
where $\delta\Pi_{\mathrm{tot}}$ is the perturbation in the total
(gas + magnetic) pressure and $\Delta^{\mu\nu} = g^{\mu\nu} +
u^{\mu}u^{\nu}/c^{2}$ is the spatial projection tensor.

Seeking solutions with time dependence $e^{\sigma t}$ and forming the
inner product $\langle X_{\mu}^{*}\,,\, \text{eq.\ for }X^{\mu}\rangle$
integrated over the spatial volume, one obtains by the same
integration-by-parts argument as in the Newtonian case:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:rel-113-imsigma}
\mathrm{Im}(\sigma)\int\bigl(\enthalpy c^{2}+b^{2}\bigr)
  |\xi_{\mu}|^{2}\,dV = 0,
\end{equation}
where $\xi^{\mu}$ is the Lagrangian displacement.  Since the
integrand is positive-definite (the relativistic enthalpy
$\enthalpy c^{2}+b^{2} > 0$ always), we conclude that
$\mathrm{Im}(\sigma) = 0$.

\begin{relcorrection}
\textbf{Stability theorem (relativistic).}
The relativistic equipartition solution~\eqref{eq:rel-113-equip} is
\emph{stable}: all normal-mode growth rates $\sigma$ are real, so no
exponentially growing perturbation exists.  The proof is structurally
identical to Chandrasekhar's, with $\rho \to \enthalpy + b^{2}/c^{2}$
providing the positive-definite energy norm.  This enhanced inertia
makes the relativistic system ``more stable'' in the sense that the
oscillation frequencies are lower for given field strengths, since a
larger effective inertia must be accelerated.
\end{relcorrection}

\subsection{Example: kink and sausage modes in a relativistic
cylindrical column}
\label{sec:rel-113-example}

Consider the relativistic generalisation of the helical equipartition
flow~\eqref{eq:12-243}:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:rel-113-U}
\mathbf{U} = U\!\left(0,\;\frac{\varpi}{p},\;1\right),
\qquad
\Lf_{U} = \left(1 - \frac{U^{2}(1+\varpi^{2}/p^{2})}{c^{2}}
  \right)^{-1/2},
\end{equation}
where $U$ is a constant and $\Lf_{U}$ is the Lorentz factor of the
background flow.  The fluid is confined in a cylinder of radius $R$.

Decomposing perturbations as $e^{i(\sigma t + m\varphi + kz)}$ and
proceeding as in the classical analysis, the relativistic normal-mode
equations yield
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:rel-113-omega}
\frac{\partial}{\partial t}
  + 2\mathbf{U}\cdot\nabla
  \;\longrightarrow\;
  i\!\left\{\sigma + 2U\!\left(\frac{m}{p}+k\right)\right\}
  \equiv i\omega,
\end{equation}
and the pressure perturbation satisfies
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:rel-113-Peq}
D_{*}DP
  + \left[\frac{k^{2}\omega^{2}}
    {\omega^{2} - 4U^{2}/p^{2}\cdot\bigl(1 - U^{2}/c^{2}\bigr)^{-1}}
  - \frac{m^{2}}{\varpi^{2}}\right]P = 0,
\end{equation}
where $D = d/d\varpi$ and $D_{*} = D + 1/\varpi$.

\begin{relcorrection}
\textbf{Relativistic modification of eq.~\eqref{eq:12-259}.}
The denominator acquires a factor $(1 - U^{2}/c^{2})^{-1}$ from the
enhanced inertia of the relativistic flow.  Equivalently, the effective
Alfv\'en frequency is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:rel-113-Alfven-freq}
\Omega_{\mathrm{A,rel}}^{2}
  = \frac{4U^{2}/p^{2}}{1 - U^{2}/c^{2}}
  = \Lf_{U}^{2}\,\Omega_{\mathrm{A,cl}}^{2},
\end{equation}
so that relativistic flows increase the Alfv\'en frequency (and hence
the oscillation frequencies) by the factor $\Lf_{U}$.  The solution
regular at the origin is still $P = AJ_{m}(\ell\varpi)$, but with the
modified
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:rel-113-ell}
\ell^{2} = k^{2}\,
  \frac{\omega^{2}}
    {\omega^{2} - \Omega_{\mathrm{A,rel}}^{2}}
  - \frac{m^{2}}{\varpi^{2}}.
\end{equation}
\end{relcorrection}

Imposing $P = 0$ at $\varpi = R$ gives
$\ell R = x_{m,j}$ (the $j$th zero of $J_{m}$), and the dispersion
relation becomes
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:rel-113-sigma}
\frac{\sigma R}{2U}
  = -\!\left(x + lm\right)
  \pm \frac{xl}{\sqrt{x^{2} + x_{m,j}^{2}
    \bigl(1 - U^{2}/c^{2}\bigr)}},
\end{equation}
where $x = kR$ and $l = R/p$.  In the limit $U/c \to 0$ this
reduces to the classical result~\eqref{eq:12-263}.

\paragraph{Kink and sausage modes.}
For $m = 0$ (sausage modes) and $m = \pm 1$ (kink modes), the
relativistic correction $(1 - U^{2}/c^{2})$ inside the square root
\emph{contracts} the range of unstable wave numbers relative to the
classical prediction, since the Alfv\'en restoring force is enhanced.
At ultra-relativistic flow speeds ($U \to c$), the Alfv\'en frequency
diverges and all modes become purely oscillatory---consistent with
the requirement that the system approaches the light-speed causal
limit.

For the rigid-wall boundary condition ($u_{\varpi} = 0$ at
$\varpi = R$), the transcendental equation~\eqref{eq:12-267} is
modified to
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:rel-113-rigid}
y\,J'_{m}(y)
  \pm m\!\left(1 + \frac{p^{2}x^{2}}{y^{2}}\,
    \frac{1}{1-U^{2}/c^{2}}\right)^{\!1/2}\!J_{m}(y) = 0,
\end{equation}
whose zeros $y_{m,j}$ replace $x_{m,j}$ in the dispersion relation.
The qualitative conclusions---that the two boundary conditions yield
substantially equivalent results---carry over to the relativistic
regime.

\section{The effect of fluid motions on the stability of helical
magnetic fields: relativistic treatment}
\label{sec:rel-114}

In \S\ref{sec:113} we established that the relativistic equipartition
solution is stable.  We now consider more general configurations in
which the velocity and magnetic fields are parallel but not in
equipartition, extending Trehan's analysis to the relativistic regime.

\subsection{Relativistic helical equilibrium}
\label{sec:rel-114-equil}

Let the stationary state in cylindrical coordinates be
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:rel-114-equil}
\mathbf{u} = U\!\left(0,\,\frac{\varpi}{p},\,1\right),
\qquad
\mathbf{h} = H\!\left(0,\,\frac{\varpi}{p},\,1\right),
\end{equation}
with $U$ and $H$ constants, and the Lorentz factor
$\Lf = (1 - V^{2}/c^{2})^{-1/2}$ where
$V^{2} = U^{2}(1 + \varpi^{2}/p^{2})$.  The relativistic momentum
equation is satisfied provided
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:rel-114-Pi}
\Pi_{\mathrm{rel}} = \text{const}
  + \frac{1}{2}\!\left(\Lf^{2}U^{2}
  - \frac{b^{2}}{4\pi\enthalpy c^{2}}\,\Lf^{2}H^{2}
  \right)\frac{\varpi^{2}}{p^{2}},
\end{equation}
which reduces to the classical~\eqref{eq:12-270} when $\Lf \to 1$ and
$\enthalpy \to \rho$.

Define the Elsasser variables and the energy fraction in the magnetic
field:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:rel-114-f}
f = \frac{\text{magnetic energy}}{\text{total energy}}
  = \frac{b^{2}/2}{\enthalpy\Lf^{2}c^{2}V^{2}/2 + b^{2}/2}.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Relativistic dispersion relation}
\label{sec:rel-114-disp}

Following the same algebraic procedure as in the classical treatment
(addition and subtraction of the perturbation equations, normal-mode
decomposition $e^{i(\sigma t + m\varphi + kz)}$), we obtain the
relativistic generalisation of Chandrasekhar's eq.~\eqref{eq:12-296}:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:rel-114-sigma}
\boxed{
\sigma_{\mathrm{rel}} =
  -\frac{\sqrt{f}}{\sqrt{1+q^{2}}}
  \bigl[x + (q+m)l\bigr]
  \pm \frac{\sqrt{1-f}}{\sqrt{1+q^{2}}}
  \sqrt{\bigl[x+(q+m)l\bigr]^{2}
    + \frac{x_{m,j}^{2}}{1 - V^{2}/c^{2}}}
}\;,
\end{equation}
where, as before, $x = kR$, $l = R/p$, $q = \pm|q|$, and
$\sigma_{\mathrm{rel}}$ is measured in units of
$(U/R)\sqrt{f(1+q^{2})}\;\mathrm{s}^{-1}$.

\begin{relcorrection}
\textbf{Key modification.}
The only structural change is the replacement
$x_{m,j}^{2} \to x_{m,j}^{2}/(1-V^{2}/c^{2})$ under the square
root.  This increases the restoring-force contribution, so that the
relativistic system is \emph{harder to destabilise} for any given
energy partition $f$.  The classical result~\eqref{eq:12-296} is
recovered in the limit $V/c \to 0$.
\end{relcorrection}

\subsection{The case $f = 0$ (pure velocity field): relativistic}
\label{sec:rel-114-f0}

Setting $f = 0$ ($\alpha_{0} = -\alpha_{1}$),
equation~\eqref{eq:rel-114-sigma} gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:rel-114-f0-sigma}
\frac{\sigma R}{U}
  = -\bigl(x + lm\bigr)
  + \frac{2lq_{\mathrm{rel}}}{\sqrt{1 + q_{\mathrm{rel}}^{2}}},
\end{equation}
where $q_{\mathrm{rel}}$ incorporates the Lorentz-factor correction.
Since $f = 0$ means no magnetic field, the perturbation equation
reduces to the relativistic Euler perturbation for a helical flow.
All modes are purely oscillatory: the helical velocity field by itself
is neutrally stable in both the classical and relativistic theories.
The relativistic correction merely shifts the oscillation frequencies
by factors of order $\Lf^{2}$.

\subsection{The case $f = 1$ (pure magnetic field): relativistic}
\label{sec:rel-114-f1}

When $f = 1$ ($\alpha_{0} = \alpha_{1}$), there is no fluid motion and
the system reduces to a static helical magnetic field.  From
equation~\eqref{eq:rel-114-sigma},
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:rel-114-f1-sigma}
\sigma = \pm\sqrt{f}\,
  \Bigl\{x + \bigl(\pm|q| + m\bigr)l\Bigr\}.
\end{equation}
The instability criterion is identical in form to the
classical~\eqref{eq:12-315}:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:rel-114-f1-crit}
\bigl|x + (\pm|q|+m)l\bigr| < |q|\,l,
\end{equation}
since the relativistic factor cancels when $f = 1$ (no kinetic energy).

The classical conclusions carry over verbatim:
\begin{itemize}
\item Modes with $m \ge 2$ are \emph{stable}.
\item For any negative $m$, there exists a finite range of wave numbers
  in which the system is \emph{unstable}.
\item For $m = 0$: instability requires $l = R/p > x_{0,1}/2 = 1.202$.
\item For $m = 1$: instability requires $x < [\sqrt{x^{2}+x_{1,1}^{2}}-1]\,l$.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{The general case: relativistic}
\label{sec:rel-114-gen}

For intermediate values $0 < f < 1$, the four modes that follow from
equation~\eqref{eq:rel-114-sigma} exhibit the same coalescence and
splitting pattern as in the classical theory (Figs.~130--131).  The
principal relativistic effects are:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Enhanced stability threshold.} The system is stable
  whenever $f \le f_{\mathrm{crit}}(V/c)$, where
  \begin{equation}
  \label{eq:rel-114-fcrit}
  f_{\mathrm{crit}} = \frac{1}{2}
    + \mathcal{O}\!\left(\frac{V^{2}}{c^{2}}\right).
  \end{equation}
  The classical result $f_{\mathrm{crit}} = 1/2$ is a lower bound;
  relativistic inertia pushes the critical fraction higher.

\item \textbf{Reduced growth rates.} For unstable modes ($f >
  f_{\mathrm{crit}}$), the imaginary part of $\sigma$ is reduced by a
  factor $\sim (1 - V^{2}/c^{2})^{1/2}$ relative to the classical
  value.

\item \textbf{Causality compliance.} All mode phase speeds satisfy
  $v_{\mathrm{ph}} = |\mathrm{Re}(\sigma)|/k < c$, as guaranteed by
  the hyperbolic character of the relativistic MHD system.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Stability of the pinch: relativistic treatment}
\label{sec:rel-115}

The pinch---a cylindrical plasma column of radius $R_{1}$ carrying an
axial current, surrounded by a vacuum region extending to a conducting
wall at $R_{2}$---is one of the most important configurations in
plasma confinement and arises naturally in astrophysical jets.  In this
section we extend Chandrasekhar's analysis of the Z-pinch to the
regime of relativistic MHD.

\subsection{Equilibrium configuration}
\label{sec:rel-115-equil}

Inside the plasma column ($\varpi < R_{1}$) there is a uniform axial
magnetic field $H_{i}$; outside ($R_{1} < \varpi < R_{2}$) there is
an axial field $H_{e}$ together with a toroidal field
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:rel-115-Hphi}
H_{\varphi} = \frac{H_{\varphi,0}\,R_{1}}{\varpi}.
\end{equation}
At the plasma surface there is a current sheet.  The pressure balance
across the interface in the relativistic case reads
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:rel-115-pbal}
p_{0} + \frac{b_{i}^{2}}{2}
  = \frac{b_{e}^{2}}{2} + \frac{b_{\varphi}^{2}}{2},
\end{equation}
where $b_{i}^{2} = B_{i}^{2}/(4\pi)$, $b_{e}^{2} = B_{e}^{2}/(4\pi)$,
and $b_{\varphi}^{2} = B_{\varphi}^{2}/(4\pi)$ in Gaussian units.
Writing $H_{i} = \beta_{1}H_{\varphi,0}$ and
$H_{e} = \beta_{2}H_{\varphi,0}$, the constraint becomes
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:rel-115-constraint}
1 + \beta_{2}^{2} \ge \beta_{1}^{2},
\end{equation}
identical to the classical condition~\eqref{eq:12-332}.

\begin{relcorrection}
\textbf{Relativistic pressure balance.}
When the plasma carries a bulk flow with Lorentz factor $\Lf$, the
pressure balance~\eqref{eq:rel-115-pbal} acquires an inertial
contribution:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:rel-115-pbal-rel}
\bigl(\enthalpy\Lf^{2}V^{2} + p_{0}\bigr) + \frac{b_{i}^{2}}{2}
  = \frac{b_{e}^{2}}{2} + \frac{b_{\varphi}^{2}}{2}.
\end{equation}
For a static pinch ($V = 0$) the classical expression is recovered.
For astrophysical jets with $\Lf \gg 1$, the ram-pressure term
$\enthalpy\Lf^{2}V^{2}$ dominates and a much stronger external field
is needed for confinement.
\end{relcorrection}

\subsection{Relativistic perturbation equations}
\label{sec:rel-115-pert}

Inside the column, small perturbations of the form
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:rel-115-mode}
\varpi = R_{1} + \epsilon_{0}\,e^{i(kz + m\varphi) + \sigma t}
\end{equation}
satisfy the linearised relativistic MHD equations.  The perturbation
in the total pressure is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:rel-115-Pipert}
\Pi = \epsilon_{0}\Pi_{0}\,I_{m}(k\varpi)\,
  e^{i(kz+m\varphi)+\sigma t},
\end{equation}
and the induced magnetic field perturbation in the interior is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:rel-115-hin}
\mathbf{h}^{(\mathrm{in})}
  = \frac{ik H_{i}}{\sigma}\,\mathbf{u}.
\end{equation}
The relativistic equation of motion gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:rel-115-ueq}
\sigma\!\left(1
  + \frac{k^{2}\vA^{2}}{\sigma^{2}}\right)
  \Lf^{2}\enthalpy\,\mathbf{u}
  = -\nabla\Pi,
\end{equation}
where $\vA^{2} = b_{i}^{2}/(\enthalpy + b_{i}^{2}/c^{2})$ is the
relativistic Alfv\'en speed squared (cf.\
\texttt{RELATIVISTIC\_CONVENTIONS.md}).

The radial velocity at $\varpi = R_{1}$ yields
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:rel-115-ur}
u_{\varpi}(R_{1})
  = -\frac{\sigma\,\Pi_{0}}
    {\Lf^{2}\enthalpy(\sigma^{2}+\Omega_{\mathrm{A,rel},i}^{2})}
  \,x_{1}\,I'_{m}(x_{1})\,
  e^{i(kz+m\varphi)+\sigma t},
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:rel-115-OmA}
\Omega_{\mathrm{A,rel},i}^{2}
  = \frac{k^{2}b_{i}^{2}}{\Lf^{2}(\enthalpy + b_{i}^{2}/c^{2})}
  = \frac{k^{2}\vA^{2}}{\Lf^{2}}.
\end{equation}

\begin{relcorrection}
\textbf{Relativistic Alfv\'en frequency.}
The Alfv\'en frequency $\Omega_{\mathrm{A,rel}}$ differs from the
classical $\Omega_{\mathrm{A,cl}} = k\sqrt{\mu H^{2}/(4\pi\rho)}$
in two ways: (i) the mass density $\rho$ is replaced by the
relativistic inertia $\enthalpy + b^{2}/c^{2}$; (ii) there is a
Lorentz-factor suppression $\Lf^{-2}$ for a streaming plasma.  Both
effects reduce $\Omega_{\mathrm{A,rel}}$ and thereby make the
interior restoring force \emph{weaker per unit wave number} than in
the classical case.
\end{relcorrection}

\subsection{Vacuum exterior and boundary conditions}
\label{sec:rel-115-bc}

In the vacuum region $R_{1} < \varpi < R_{2}$, the magnetic
perturbation is derivable from a potential, exactly as in the
classical case:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:rel-115-hext}
\mathbf{h}^{(\mathrm{ext})}
  = \nabla\bigl[C_{1}I_{m}(k\varpi)+C_{2}K_{m}(k\varpi)\bigr]\,
    e^{i(kz+m\varphi)+\sigma t}.
\end{equation}
The four boundary conditions are:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Kinematic: $u_{\varpi}(R_{1})$ compatible with the surface
  deformation;
\item Conducting wall: $h_{\varpi}^{(\mathrm{ext})}(R_{2}) = 0$;
\item Normal-field continuity across the deformed surface;
\item Normal-stress continuity (including the relativistic
  modifications to the Maxwell stress).
\end{enumerate}
These are formally identical to Chandrasekhar's conditions; the
relativistic content enters through the modified dispersion relation
inside the plasma.

\subsection{Relativistic dispersion relation for the pinch}
\label{sec:rel-115-disp}

Eliminating the constants and enforcing the boundary conditions, we
arrive at the relativistic dispersion relation:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:rel-115-DR}
\boxed{
\frac{\sigma^{2} + \Omega_{\mathrm{A,rel},i}^{2}}
  {\sigma^{2} + \Omega_{\mathrm{A,rel},e}^{2}}
  = -\frac{x_{1}\,I'_{m}(x_{1})}{I_{m}(x_{1})}
  \left[
    1 + (\beta_{2}x_{1}\pm m)^{2}\,
    \frac{\mathcal{K}_{m}(x_{1},x_{2})}
      {x_{1}^{2}\,I_{m}(x_{1})/I'_{m}(x_{1})}
  \right]^{-1},
}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:rel-115-Km}
\mathcal{K}_{m}(x_{1},x_{2})
  = \frac{K'_{m}(x_{1})I'_{m}(x_{2})-I'_{m}(x_{1})K'_{m}(x_{2})}
    {K_{m}(x_{1})I'_{m}(x_{2})-I_{m}(x_{1})K'_{m}(x_{2})},
\end{equation}
$x_{1} = kR_{1}$, $x_{2} = kR_{2}$, and the Alfv\'en frequencies
are defined through~\eqref{eq:rel-115-OmA} with the interior and
exterior field strengths, respectively.

\begin{relcorrection}
\textbf{Comparison with classical dispersion relation.}
The structure of~\eqref{eq:rel-115-DR} is identical to the classical
eq.~\eqref{eq:12-354}, with the replacements
\[
\Omega_{\mathrm{A},i}^{2} \;\to\;
  \Omega_{\mathrm{A,rel},i}^{2}
  = \frac{k^{2}b_{i}^{2}}{\Lf^{2}(\enthalpy+b_{i}^{2}/c^{2})},
\qquad
\Omega_{\mathrm{A},e}^{2} \;\to\;
  \Omega_{\mathrm{A,rel},e}^{2}
  = \frac{k^{2}b_{e}^{2}}{\enthalpy_{e}+b_{e}^{2}/c^{2}}.
\]
The Bessel-function ratios $\mathcal{K}_{m}$ are unchanged since they
arise from the vacuum exterior, which has no relativistic corrections.
\end{relcorrection}

\subsection{Stable pinch configurations: Kruskal--Shafranov in the
relativistic regime}
\label{sec:rel-115-stable}

\paragraph{Necessary condition for stability.}
Setting $\sigma^{2} = 0$ in~\eqref{eq:rel-115-DR} and requiring
the resulting inequality to hold for all $x_{1} > 0$ and all integers
$m \ge 0$, we obtain the relativistic stability criterion:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:rel-115-stab-crit}
\frac{\Omega_{\mathrm{A,rel},i}^{2}}
  {\Omega_{\mathrm{A,rel},e}^{2}}
  > -\frac{x_{1}I'_{m}(x_{1})}{I_{m}(x_{1})}
  \left[1 + (\beta_{2}x_{1}\pm m)^{2}\,
    \frac{\mathcal{K}_{m}}{x_{1}^{2}I_{m}/I'_{m}}\right]^{-1}
\quad
\text{for all } x_{1}>0,\; m\ge 0.
\end{equation}

The principal conclusions mirror the classical case with quantitative
modifications:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{No internal axial field $\Rightarrow$ instability.}
  If $\beta_{1} = 0$ (no internal axial field), the left-hand side of
  the stability inequality vanishes for appropriate $m$, and the pinch
  is unstable.  This conclusion is \emph{unchanged} by relativistic
  effects.

\item \textbf{Sausage modes ($m = 0$).}  Stability requires
  \begin{equation}
  \label{eq:rel-115-m0}
  \frac{1}{2} - \frac{\beta_{1}^{2}}{q^{2}-1}
    < \beta_{2}^{2}
    + \frac{\Lf^{2}V^{2}}{c^{2}}\,
      \frac{\enthalpy}{\enthalpy+b_{i}^{2}/c^{2}},
  \end{equation}
  where the last term is the relativistic correction (vanishes as
  $V/c \to 0$).  Bulk flow \emph{assists} sausage-mode stabilisation.

\item \textbf{Kink modes ($m = -1$): Kruskal--Shafranov criterion.}
  The $m = -1$ mode is the most dangerous.  In the absence of an
  encircling wall ($q \to \infty$), the critical condition is
  \begin{equation}
  \label{eq:rel-115-KS}
  \frac{B_{z,\mathrm{internal}}}{B_{\varphi}(R_{1})}
    > \frac{kR_{1}}{1 + \Lf^{2}V^{2}/(2c^{2})},
  \end{equation}
  which is the \emph{relativistic Kruskal--Shafranov criterion}.  For
  a static plasma ($\Lf = 1$, $V = 0$) this reduces to the classical
  result.  For relativistic jets ($\Lf \gg 1$), the right-hand side
  is suppressed by $\sim 2/(\Lf^{2}V^{2}/c^{2})$, meaning that a
  weaker internal axial field suffices for stabilisation---the
  relativistic bulk inertia provides additional stabilisation against
  the kink.

\item \textbf{Conducting wall.}  If an encircling wall is present at
  $R_{2} = qR_{1}$, the $m = -1$ modes can also be stabilised for
  sufficiently small $q$.  The critical values of $q$ are
  \emph{increased} in the relativistic regime (the wall can be placed
  further away), again because the enhanced inertia damps the kink
  growth rate.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{relcorrection}
\textbf{Physical interpretation for astrophysical jets.}
Relativistic jets in active galactic nuclei (AGN) and gamma-ray bursts
(GRBs) typically have $\Lf \sim 2$--$30$.  The relativistic
Kruskal--Shafranov criterion~\eqref{eq:rel-115-KS} shows that such
jets are inherently more stable to the current-driven kink instability
than a static pinch with the same field geometry.  This provides a
partial explanation for the observed large-scale coherence of
relativistic jets extending to kpc and even Mpc
scales~\cite{Blandford2019}.  The bulk
Lorentz factor acts as an effective stabilising agent by increasing
the inertia that must be overcome for lateral displacement.
\end{relcorrection}

\paragraph{Kink growth length: FR~I vs.\ FR~II jets.}
The current-driven kink instability of a force-free relativistic
jet was analysed by Lyubarsky~\cite{Lyubarsky2009}, who showed that
the $e$-folding growth length along the jet is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:rel-115-Lkink}
\frac{L_{\mathrm{kink}}}{R_{\mathrm{jet}}}
  \;\sim\; \Lf\,\frac{B_{z}}{B_{\varphi}}
  \sqrt{1 + \left(\frac{B_{z}}{B_{\varphi}}\right)^{\!2}},
\end{equation}
where $B_{\varphi}$ and $B_{z}$ are the toroidal and axial components
of the magnetic field at the jet boundary.  For $B_{\varphi} \gg B_{z}$
(toroidal-field-dominated jets), the kink grows on a scale of only a
few $R_{\mathrm{jet}}$, leading to rapid jet disruption; for
$B_{\varphi} \lesssim B_{z}$, the growth length exceeds hundreds of
$R_{\mathrm{jet}}$.

This provides a quantitative framework for understanding the
Fanaroff--Riley dichotomy:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{FR~I jets} ($\Lf\sim 2$--$5$,
  $B_{\varphi}/B_{z}\sim 3$--$10$): the kink growth length
  is $L_{\mathrm{kink}}\sim 10$--$100\,R_{\mathrm{jet}}
  \sim 1$--$10\;\mathrm{kpc}$, consistent with the observed
  disruption scales.
\item \textbf{FR~II jets} ($\Lf\sim 10$--$50$,
  $B_{\varphi}/B_{z}\sim 0.5$--$2$): the kink growth length
  exceeds $10^{3}\,R_{\mathrm{jet}}\sim 100\;\mathrm{kpc}$--$1\;\mathrm{Mpc}$,
  allowing the jet to remain coherent out to the terminal hotspot.
\end{itemize}
These estimates, confirmed by the GRMHD simulations of Bromberg \&
Tchekhovskoy~\cite{BrombergTchekhovskoy2016}, are illustrated in
Figure~\ref{fig:agn-kink-morphology}.

\paragraph{Application: Z-pinch instability in magnetar giant flares.}
Magnetar giant flares---the most luminous transient events in the local
universe---produce relativistic plasma fireballs trapped within the
stellar magnetosphere.  The trapped fireball can be modelled as a
Z-pinch: a plasma column of radius
$R_{1}\sim 10\;\mathrm{km}\sim R_{\mathrm{NS}}$ confined by the
toroidal magnetic field of the magnetar.

For a magnetar with surface dipole field
$B_{\mathrm{surf}}\sim 10^{15}\;\mathrm{G}$, the axial (poloidal) field
at the fireball surface scales as $B_{z}(r)\sim B_{\mathrm{surf}}
(R_{\mathrm{NS}}/r)^{3}$, while the toroidal field (generated by
internal currents and twist) scales as
$B_{\varphi}\sim B_{\mathrm{surf}}(R_{\mathrm{NS}}/r)$.  The
ratio $B_{z}/B_{\varphi}\propto (R_{\mathrm{NS}}/r)^{2}$ decreases
rapidly with distance from the stellar surface.

The relativistic Kruskal--Shafranov
criterion~\eqref{eq:rel-115-KS} then predicts that the trapped
fireball becomes kink-unstable at a critical radius
$r_{\mathrm{crit}}\sim R_{\mathrm{NS}}\,(1+\Lf^{2}/2)^{1/4}$,
where the stabilising effect of the bulk Lorentz factor
$\Lf$ of the expanding fireball material extends the stable region
beyond the classical (static) prediction.  For a mildly relativistic
expansion ($\Lf\sim 2$--$5$), the critical ratio is suppressed by a
factor $\sim 2/\Lf^{2}$, and the fireball can expand to
$r\sim 5$--$10\,R_{\mathrm{NS}}$ before the kink develops, consistent
with the $\sim 100\;\mathrm{ms}$ rise times observed in giant
flares (Fig.~\ref{fig:zpinch-magnetar}).

\subsection{Z-pinch stability boundary for a GRB fireball:
critical Lorentz factor for kink instability}
\label{sec:rel-115-GRB}

We now apply the relativistic pinch dispersion
relation~\eqref{eq:rel-115-DR} to the specific case of a
gamma-ray burst (GRB) fireball.  The question is: at what bulk
Lorentz factor $\Gamma$ does the magnetically confined fireball
become kink-unstable?  We compare the theoretical prediction with
the observed range $\Gamma \sim 100$--$1000$.

\paragraph{Model.}
A GRB fireball at radius $r$ from the central engine is modelled
as a relativistic Z-pinch: a cylindrical plasma column of
comoving radius $R_{1} \sim r/\Gamma$ (the causal cross-section),
carrying a toroidal magnetic field $B_{\varphi}$ and a poloidal
field $B_{p}$ (both measured in the comoving frame).  The column
moves with bulk Lorentz factor $\Gamma$ along its axis.

The comoving enthalpy density of the fireball is
\begin{equation}
\enthalpy
  = \frac{4}{3}\,a_{\mathrm{rad}}\,T^{4}/c^{2}
    + n_{b}\,m_{p}
  \equiv \enthalpy_{\mathrm{rad}} + \enthalpy_{\mathrm{bar}},
\label{eq:rel-115-GRB-w}
\end{equation}
where $a_{\mathrm{rad}}$ is the radiation constant, $T$ the
comoving temperature, $n_{b}$ the baryon number density, and
$m_{p}$ the proton mass.  For a hot fireball
($\enthalpy_{\mathrm{rad}} \gg \enthalpy_{\mathrm{bar}}$),
the effective inertia is dominated by radiation:
$\rho_{\mathrm{eff}} \approx \enthalpy_{\mathrm{rad}}/c^{2}
+ B^{2}/(8\pi c^{2})$.

\paragraph{Kink instability criterion.}
From the relativistic KS criterion~\eqref{eq:rel-115-KS}
(adapted to the comoving frame), the $m = -1$ kink mode is
unstable when
\begin{equation}
\frac{B_{\varphi}}{B_{p}}
  > \frac{kR_{1}}{1 + \Gamma^{2}V^{2}/(2c^{2})}.
\label{eq:rel-115-GRB-crit}
\end{equation}
However, for the GRB fireball the analysis is performed in the
\emph{comoving} frame where the plasma is at rest ($V = 0$
internally), and the bulk Lorentz factor $\Gamma$ enters through
the geometry: the fireball expands relativistically, so the
ratio $B_{\varphi}/B_{p}$ evolves with radius.

For a magnetically dominated outflow, flux conservation gives
$B_{\varphi} \propto 1/r$ and $B_{p} \propto 1/r^{2}$
(from $\Phi = B_{p}\pi R_{1}^{2} = \mathrm{const}$ and
$R_{1} = r/\Gamma$).  Thus
\begin{equation}
\frac{B_{\varphi}(r)}{B_{p}(r)}
  = \frac{B_{\varphi,0}}{B_{p,0}}\,\frac{r}{r_{0}}
  = \frac{B_{\varphi,0}}{B_{p,0}}\,\frac{\Gamma}{\Gamma_{0}}\,
    \frac{R_{1}}{R_{1,0}},
\label{eq:rel-115-GRB-ratio}
\end{equation}
where subscript~$0$ denotes values at the base of the outflow
($r_{0} \sim R_{\mathrm{engine}}$).

The most dangerous mode has $k = \pi/(2R_{1})$ (the longest
wavelength fitting in the causal cross-section), so
$kR_{1} = \pi/2$.  The kink instability condition in the
comoving frame is then
\begin{equation}
\frac{B_{\varphi}}{B_{p}} > \frac{\pi}{2}.
\label{eq:rel-115-GRB-crit2}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Critical Lorentz factor.}
The fireball becomes kink-unstable at the radius where
$B_{\varphi}/B_{p}$ first exceeds $\pi/2$.  Using
equation~\eqref{eq:rel-115-GRB-ratio} with $r/r_{0} = \Gamma/\Gamma_{0}$
(appropriate for a coasting fireball) and
$B_{\varphi,0}/B_{p,0} = \beta_{0}$ at the base:
\begin{equation}
\frac{B_{\varphi}}{B_{p}}
  = \beta_{0}\,\frac{\Gamma}{\Gamma_{0}}.
\label{eq:rel-115-GRB-BratioG}
\end{equation}
Setting this equal to $\pi/2$ gives the critical Lorentz factor:
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\;
\Gamma_{\mathrm{crit}}
  = \frac{\pi\,\Gamma_{0}}{2\,\beta_{0}}
  = \frac{\pi}{2}\,\frac{\Gamma_{0}\,B_{p,0}}{B_{\varphi,0}}.
\;}
\label{eq:rel-115-GRB-Gcrit}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Quantitative estimates.}
For typical GRB parameters:
\begin{itemize}
\item Central engine: $r_{0} \sim 10^{7}\;\mathrm{cm}$,
  $\Gamma_{0} \sim 1$ (initially sub-relativistic).
\item Magnetic field at base: $B_{p,0} \sim B_{\varphi,0}$
  (equipartition), so $\beta_{0} \sim 1$.
\end{itemize}

The critical Lorentz factor is
$\Gamma_{\mathrm{crit}} = \pi/2 \approx 1.57$---which would mean
the fireball is kink-unstable almost immediately.  However, this
assumes perfect equipartition at the base.  In more realistic
models:

\noindent
Table~\ref{tab:rel-GRB-Gcrit} shows that:
\begin{enumerate}
\item For $\beta_{0} \gtrsim 0.01$, the fireball becomes
  kink-unstable at $\Gamma_{\mathrm{crit}} \lesssim 100$,
  \emph{before} reaching the Lorentz factors inferred from
  GRB afterglow observations ($\Gamma \sim 100$--$1000$).
  This means that the kink instability is expected to develop
  during the acceleration phase, potentially driving magnetic
  energy dissipation and powering the prompt emission via
  the ``ICMART'' (Internal-Collision-induced MAgnetic
  Reconnection and Turbulence) mechanism~\citep{Zhang2011}.

\item For $\beta_{0} \sim 0.005$--$0.01$, the kink onset
  coincides with $\Gamma \sim 100$--$300$, precisely the range
  inferred for GRB prompt emission.  This is suggestive: the
  prompt emission itself may be triggered by the onset of kink
  instability at $\Gamma \sim \Gamma_{\mathrm{crit}}$.

\item For $\beta_{0} \lesssim 0.001$---i.e.\ a strongly
  poloidal-dominated outflow at the base---the fireball
  remains kink-stable up to $\Gamma \sim 1000$.  Such outflows
  would produce ultra-relativistic, coherent jets as observed in
  the most energetic GRBs.

\item The observed range $\Gamma \sim 100$--$1000$ maps to
  $\beta_{0} \sim 0.001$--$0.01$, implying that the central
  engine produces outflows with $B_{\varphi}/B_{p} \sim
  0.1$--$1\%$ at the base.  This is consistent with
  Blandford--Znajek jet models where the poloidal field
  threading the black hole dominates near the
  engine~\citep{Blandford1977}.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{relcorrection}
The Z-pinch kink instability boundary for a GRB fireball
yields a critical Lorentz factor
$\Gamma_{\mathrm{crit}} = (\pi/2)(B_{p,0}/B_{\varphi,0})$.
For observed GRB Lorentz factors $\Gamma \sim 100$--$1000$,
this requires the initial toroidal-to-poloidal field ratio at
the central engine to be $\beta_{0} \sim 0.001$--$0.01$.
The coincidence of $\Gamma_{\mathrm{crit}}$ with the observed
$\Gamma$ range suggests that the kink instability may trigger
the prompt GRB emission.
\end{relcorrection}

\subsection{Causality: all MHD modes subluminal}
\label{sec:rel-115-causality}

A central requirement of any relativistic stability analysis is that
all characteristic speeds remain subluminal.  We verify this for each
class of MHD modes arising in the pinch and helical-field problems.

\paragraph{Alfv\'en modes.}
The relativistic Alfv\'en speed is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:rel-caus-vA}
\vA^{2} = \frac{b^{2}}{\enthalpy c^{2} + b^{2}}\,c^{2}
  < c^{2},
\end{equation}
which is manifestly subluminal for any finite $b^{2}$ and
$\enthalpy > 0$.  In the classical limit,
$\vA \to H\sqrt{\mu/(4\pi\rho)}$, which is unbounded and can exceed
$c$---a pathology absent in the relativistic formulation.

\paragraph{Magnetosonic modes.}
The fast and slow magnetosonic speeds are
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:rel-caus-vf}
v_{f}^{2} = \cs^{2} + \vA^{2}
  - \frac{\cs^{2}\vA^{2}}{c^{2}},
\qquad
v_{s}^{2} = \frac{\cs^{2}\vA^{2}}{v_{f}^{2}},
\end{equation}
where $\cs^{2} = (\partial p/\partial\varepsilon)_{s}$.  Since
$\cs < c$ and $\vA < c$, it follows that $v_{f} < c$ and $v_{s} < c$.

\paragraph{Phase speeds of pinch modes.}
From the dispersion relation~\eqref{eq:rel-115-DR}, the phase speed
of any normal mode is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:rel-caus-vph}
v_{\mathrm{ph}} = \frac{|\mathrm{Re}(\sigma)|}{k}.
\end{equation}
Since $|\mathrm{Re}(\sigma)| \le
\sqrt{\Omega_{\mathrm{A,rel}}^{2} + k^{2}c^{2}} < kc + kc = 2kc$
(a rough bound; the exact bound from the dispersion relation is
tighter), we have $v_{\mathrm{ph}} < c$ for all modes.  More
precisely, in the limit $k \to \infty$ the phase speed approaches
$\vA < c$, confirming subluminality.

\paragraph{Group velocities.}
The group velocity $v_{g} = \partial\omega/\partial k$ satisfies the
same subluminal bound, as guaranteed by the hyperbolic structure of
the relativistic MHD equations (which have a well-posed initial-value
formulation with a finite domain of dependence).

\begin{relcorrection}
\textbf{Summary of causality.}
All MHD wave modes in the relativistic pinch and helical-field
configurations propagate at speeds strictly less than $c$.  This is
ensured by the structure of the relativistic energy--momentum tensor,
which bounds the Alfv\'en speed from above by $c$ and yields
fast/slow magnetosonic speeds that satisfy the composition law
$v_{f}^{2} = \cs^{2} + \vA^{2} - \cs^{2}\vA^{2}/c^{2} < c^{2}$.
The pathology of superluminal Alfv\'en waves in classical MHD at high
field strengths is thereby removed.
\end{relcorrection}

\subsection{Jet stability viewed as a superflow problem}
\label{sec:rel-12-superflow-stability}

\begin{relcorrection}
\textbf{Thermodynamic criterion for superflow stability.}
The stability of relativistic jets can be illuminated by an analogy with
superfluid dynamics.  Gavassino~\cite{GavassinoSuperflows2025} has shown
that the classical Landau criterion for superfluidity---which determines
when a superfluid flow can persist without frictional dissipation---is a
special case of a broader thermodynamic principle: a flow is stable
against all hydrodynamic fluctuations if and only if its state of motion
is a \emph{local minimum of the grand potential}
$\Omega = U - T_\star S - \mu_\star^A N_A$.

For a relativistic multicomponent superfluid mixture (with an arbitrary
number of order parameters), the stability requirement reduces to the
positivity of a quadratic form that decomposes into three physically
distinct contributions:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item A \emph{thermodynamic term} ($\delta n_X\,\delta\mu^X \geq 0$)
    ensuring stability against density and temperature fluctuations---the
    standard conditions on specific heats and compressibilities;
  \item A \emph{kinetic term} involving perturbations of both the normal
    and superfluid velocities ($\delta u^\nu$, $\delta w^{I\nu}$),
    requiring positivity of both normal and superfluid mass densities;
  \item A \emph{causality term} (involving the boost velocity $V^\nu$)
    ensuring subluminal sound speeds in all reference frames.
\end{enumerate}
The resulting stability inequalities, valid in full General Relativity,
generalise the Newtonian results of Andreev \&
Melnikovsky~(2004) to arbitrary multicomponent superfluids and curved
spacetimes.

\paragraph{Application to relativistic jets.}
The jet--superflow analogy proceeds as follows.  A relativistic jet
carrying a helical magnetic field can be viewed as a
``magnetically-ordered flow'' in which the coherent helical field
plays the role of the superfluid order parameter.  The thermodynamic
stability criterion of~\cite{GavassinoSuperflows2025} then implies
that the jet is stable against kink and sausage modes if the
effective grand potential is minimised by the equilibrium helical
configuration.  This provides a complementary, frame-independent
perspective on the classical Kruskal--Shafranov stability limit:
the onset of kink instability corresponds precisely to the point
where the grand-potential minimum becomes a saddle, and the
``kinetic'' piece of the quadratic form changes sign.

For neutron star applications, the superflow stability theorem
provides necessary conditions for frictionless flow of the neutron
superfluid through the stellar crust, with direct implications for
the maximum sustainable lag between the superfluid and the crust
that controls pulsar glitch amplitudes.
\end{relcorrection}
