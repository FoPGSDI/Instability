\section{Relativistic onset of overstability in a rotating layer (\S29 -- relativistic)}\label{sec:rel-3-29}

We return to the question set aside in the relativistic treatment of
\S\,26: whether instability can manifest as oscillations of increasing
amplitude -- \emph{overstability} -- when the dissipative fluxes obey
the BDNK causal first-order constitutive relations rather than the
instantaneous Fourier/Navier--Stokes relations.

\subsection{Perturbation equations with rotation: Israel--Stewart
vs.\ BDNK}

For comparison with the BDNK first-order theory, we first present
the Israel--Stewart relaxation framework, in which the linearised
heat-flux perturbation $\delta q^{\mu}$ satisfies the relaxation
equation
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rel-3-IS-heat}
  \tauq\,\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\,\delta q^{\mu}
  + \delta q^{\mu}
  = -\thermcond\,\Delta^{\mu\nu}\,\partial_{\nu}(\delta T)
  - \thermcond\,T\,\frac{u^{\mu}}{c^{2}}\,
    \frac{\partial}{\partial t}(\delta T),
\end{equation}
where $\tauq > 0$ is the thermal relaxation time and $\thermcond$ is
the thermal conductivity.  Similarly, the shear-stress perturbation
$\delta\pi^{\mu\nu}$ obeys
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rel-3-IS-shear}
  \taupi\,\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\,\delta\pi^{\mu\nu}
  + \delta\pi^{\mu\nu}
  = 2\,\shearvisc\,\delta\sigma^{\mu\nu},
\end{equation}
where $\taupi > 0$ is the viscous relaxation time and $\shearvisc$ is
the shear viscosity.

In the Boussinesq-like limit appropriate to a thin layer with
$d \ll c/\Omega$, the spatial perturbation equations for
$(W, Z, \Theta)$ -- the vertical velocity, vertical vorticity, and
temperature perturbations -- take the form analogous to
equations~(93)--(95) of Ch.\,III but with the replacement
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rel-3-sigma-repl}
  \sigma \;\longrightarrow\; \hat{\sigma}(\sigma)
  \;=\; \frac{\sigma}{1 + \tauq\,\sigma}, \qquad
  p\,\sigma \;\longrightarrow\;
  \frac{p\,\sigma}{1 + \taupi\,\sigma},
\end{equation}
in the thermal and viscous diffusion operators, respectively.
Here $\sigma$ is the temporal growth/oscillation rate defined by
$\propto e^{\sigma t}$.

The key consequence is that the characteristic equation, which is
cubic in $\sigma$ classically (cf.\ equation~\eqref{eq:rel-3-char-classical}),
remains \emph{cubic} in the BDNK first-order formalism (unlike the
Israel--Stewart formulation, which would produce a quintic).

\subsection{The characteristic equation for two free boundaries}

For the lowest mode $W = W_{0}\sin\pi z$ between two free boundaries,
the classical dispersion relation~(213) becomes, after the
substitutions~\eqref{eq:rel-3-sigma-repl},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rel-3-char-IS}
\boxed{
  \bigl(\pi^{2}+a^{2}+\hat{p}\sigma\bigr)
  \Bigl[
    \bigl(\pi^{2}+a^{2}+\hat{\sigma}\bigr)^{2}
    (\pi^{2}+a^{2})
    + \pi^{2}\mathscr{T}_{\mathrm{rel}}
  \Bigr]
  = \Rarel\,a^{2}\bigl(\pi^{2}+a^{2}+\hat{\sigma}\bigr),
}
\end{equation}
where we use the shorthand
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rel-3-hatsigma-def}
  \hat{\sigma} \equiv \frac{\sigma}{1+\tauq\,\sigma}, \qquad
  \hat{p}\sigma \equiv \frac{p\,\sigma}{1+\taupi\,\sigma},
\end{equation}
and the relativistic Taylor and Rayleigh numbers are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rel-3-TaRa}
  \mathscr{T}_{\mathrm{rel}}
  = \frac{4\Omega^{2}d^{4}}{\nu^{2}}
    \relcorr{\frac{\Omega^{2}d^{2}}{c^{2}}},
  \qquad
  \Rarel
  = \frac{\alpha g\,\Delta T\,d^{3}}{\nu\kappa_{T}}
    \relcorr{\frac{p_0}{\rdensity c^{2}}}.
\end{equation}

Clearing denominators by multiplying through by
$(1+\tauq\,\sigma)^{2}(1+\taupi\,\sigma)$, the characteristic
equation~\eqref{eq:rel-3-char-IS} becomes a polynomial equation of
degree~\textbf{three} in~$\sigma$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rel-3-cubic}
  \mathcal{P}_{3}(\sigma)
  \;=\; \sum_{n=0}^{3} c_{n}\,\sigma^{n} \;=\; 0,
\end{equation}
where the coefficients $c_{n}$ depend on $a^{2}$, $\mathscr{T}_{\mathrm{rel}}$,
$\Rarel$, and $p$.  Unlike the Israel--Stewart formulation (which
produces a quintic with two spurious relaxation modes), the BDNK
first-order theory yields only the three physical hydrodynamic modes.

\begin{relcorrection}
\textbf{Classical limit.}\quad
In the BDNK first-order theory, $\hat{\sigma} = \sigma$ and
$\hat{p}\sigma = p\sigma$ identically, so the cubic~\eqref{eq:rel-3-cubic}
is precisely the Chandrasekhar cubic~(241) with the relativistic
corrections to $\Rarel$ and $\mathscr{T}_{\mathrm{rel}}$.  No spurious
relaxation modes appear.
\end{relcorrection}

\subsection{Oscillatory solutions: $\sigma = i\omega + \gamma$}

We seek solutions of~\eqref{eq:rel-3-cubic} of the form
$\sigma = i\omega + \gamma$ with $\omega$ real (oscillation frequency)
and $\gamma$ real (growth rate).  The marginal overstable state
corresponds to $\gamma = 0$, i.e.\ $\sigma = i\omega$.

Substituting $\sigma = i\omega$ into~\eqref{eq:rel-3-cubic} and
separating real and imaginary parts yields two coupled algebraic
equations in $(\omega^{2}, \Rarel)$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rel-3-omega-eq}
  \Rarel = \frac{1+x}{x}\,
  \biggl\{
    (1+x)^{2} - p\,\tilde{\omega}^{2}
    + \frac{T_{1,\mathrm{rel}}}{1+x}\,
      \frac{(1+x)^{2}+p\,\tilde{\omega}^{2}}
           {(1+x)^{2}+\tilde{\omega}^{2}}
  \biggr\}
  + \order{\frac{p_{0}}{\rdensity c^{2}}},
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rel-3-freq-eq}
  (1+x)(1+p) = T_{1,\mathrm{rel}}\,
  \frac{1-p}{(1+x)^{2}+\tilde{\omega}^{2}}
  + \tauq\,\omega^{2}\,\frac{(1+x)^{2}-\tilde{\omega}^{2}}
       {(1+x)^{2}+\tilde{\omega}^{2}}
  + \order{\tau_{q}^{2}\omega^{4}},
\end{equation}
where $\tilde{\omega}$ is the \emph{effective} oscillation frequency
in the BDNK framework,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rel-3-omegatilde}
  \tilde{\omega}^{2}
  = \frac{\omega^{2}}{1 + \tauq^{2}\omega^{2}},
\end{equation}
and $x = a^{2}/\pi^{2}$, $T_{1,\mathrm{rel}} = \mathscr{T}_{\mathrm{rel}}/\pi^{4}$
as before.

In the non-relativistic limit $\tauq\to 0$, we have
$\tilde{\omega}\to\omega$ and equations~\eqref{eq:rel-3-omega-eq}--\eqref{eq:rel-3-freq-eq}
reduce to the classical pair~(217)--(218).

\subsection{Conditions for overstability}

Solving~\eqref{eq:rel-3-freq-eq} for $\tilde{\omega}^{2}$ gives the
relativistic generalisation of~(221):
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rel-3-omega2-sol}
  \tilde{\omega}^{2}
  = \frac{T_{1,\mathrm{rel}}}{1+x}\,\frac{1-p}{1+p}
    - (1+x)^{2}
    + \tauq\,\omega^{2}\,\frac{1}{1+p}\,\frac{(1+x)^{2}-\tilde{\omega}^{2}}
         {(1+x)^{2}+\tilde{\omega}^{2}}.
\end{equation}

For $\tilde{\omega}^{2}>0$ (a necessary condition for overstable
solutions to exist), we require
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rel-3-overstab-cond}
  T_{1,\mathrm{rel}} > \frac{1+p}{1-p}\,(1+x)^{3}
  - \frac{\tauq\,\omega^{2}(1+x)}{1-p}\,
    \frac{(1+x)^{2}-\tilde{\omega}^{2}}
         {(1+x)^{2}+\tilde{\omega}^{2}}.
\end{equation}
As in the classical case, overstability is excluded for $p > 1$.
The BDNK correction (second term on the right) modifies
the threshold Taylor number at $\order{\tauq\,\omega^{2}}$ but does
not change this qualitative conclusion.

\subsubsection*{Critical Prandtl number $p^{*}$}

The critical Prandtl number $p^{*}$ at which
$R_{1,\min}^{(c)} = R_{1,\min}^{(o)}$ as $T_{1}\to\infty$
acquires a relativistic correction:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rel-3-pstar}
  p^{*}_{\mathrm{rel}}
  = 0.67659
    + c_{\mathrm{fr}}\,
      \frac{p_{0}}{\rdensity c^{2}}
    + \order{\left(\frac{p_{0}}{\rdensity c^{2}}\right)^{2}},
\end{equation}
where $c_{\mathrm{fr}}$ is a dimensionless coefficient determined by the
BDNK frame coefficients.  The leading term is the classical value~(233).

\subsection{Comparison with classical: BDNK vs.\ Israel--Stewart modes}

The cubic~\eqref{eq:rel-3-cubic} has three roots.  In the regime
$\vA \ll c$ and $v \ll c$, the roots are:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Three hydrodynamic modes} -- these are continuous
    deformations of the classical cubic roots.  They govern the
    onset of stationary convection ($\sigma_{\mathrm{real}} = 0$) or
    overstability ($\sigma = \pm i\omega$).
\end{enumerate}
Unlike the Israel--Stewart formulation (which produces a quintic with
two additional relaxation modes at $\sigma \approx -1/\tauq$ and
$\sigma \approx -1/\taupi$), the BDNK first-order theory yields
\emph{no spurious relaxation modes}.

The three hydrodynamic-mode eigenvalues acquire perturbative corrections
at $\order{p_{0}/(\rdensity c^{2})}$.

\section{Relativistic generalisation of the variational principle for
  discriminating marginal-state character (\S30 -- relativistic)}
\label{sec:rel-3-30}

\subsection{Formulation of the variational problem}

In the classical theory (\S\,30), the variational functional~(257) was
used to determine $R$ as an extremal over trial functions $W$, $Z$
satisfying the boundary conditions.  The expression involves an
integral containing $i\sigma$ linearly in the denominator, so that
the Rayleigh number is in general complex; the requirement of reality
selects the physical oscillation frequency.

For the BDNK theory, we replace $\sigma$ in the classical
equations~(248)--(250) according to~\eqref{eq:rel-3-sigma-repl}.
Defining
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rel-3-F-def}
  F_{\mathrm{rel}}
  = (D^{2}-a^{2})
    \!\left(D^{2}-a^{2}-\frac{i\sigma}{1+\tauq\,i\sigma}\right)\!W
  - \frac{2\Omega}{\nu}\,d^{2}\,DZ,
\end{equation}
the relativistic analogues of~(249) and~(250) read
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rel-3-Z-eq}
  \left(D^{2}-a^{2}-\frac{i\sigma}{1+\taupi\,i\sigma}\right)Z
  = -\frac{2\Omega}{\nu}\,d\,DW,
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rel-3-F-eq}
  \left(D^{2}-a^{2}-\frac{ip\sigma}{1+\taupi\,i\sigma}\right)F_{\mathrm{rel}}
  = -\Rarel\,a^{2}\,W.
\end{equation}

\subsection{The variational functional}

Following the same integration-by-parts procedure as in the classical
\S\,30\,$(a)$, we obtain the relativistic variational expression
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rel-3-Rvar}
  \Rarel
  = \frac{\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\!
      \bigl[(DF_{\mathrm{rel}})^{2}
        + a^{2}F_{\mathrm{rel}}^{2}
        + i\hat{p}\sigma\,F_{\mathrm{rel}}^{2}
      \bigr]\,dz}
    {a^{2}\!\left[
      \displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\!\bigl\{
        [(D^{2}-a^{2})W]^{2}
        + d^{2}[(DZ)^{2}+a^{2}Z^{2}]
      \bigr\}\,dz
      \;+\; i\hat{\sigma}
      \displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\!\bigl\{
        (DW)^{2}+a^{2}W^{2}+d^{2}Z^{2}
      \bigr\}\,dz
    \right]},
\end{equation}
where $\hat{\sigma}$ and $\hat{p}\sigma$ are defined
in~\eqref{eq:rel-3-hatsigma-def}.

\begin{relcorrection}
\textbf{Extremal but not minimal.}\quad
As in the classical case, $\delta\Rarel = 0$ for all admissible
variations $\delta F_{\mathrm{rel}}$ if and only if
equation~\eqref{eq:rel-3-F-eq} is satisfied.  The characteristic values
of $\Rarel$ have an extremal character but are \emph{not} necessarily
minimal, since $\Rarel$ can be complex.  The BDNK
modification does not alter this structural feature; it only changes
the numerical values of the extrema.
\end{relcorrection}

\subsection{Boundary conditions and the character of onset}

The boundary conditions~(252) are unaffected by the relativistic
extension: $W = F_{\mathrm{rel}} = 0$ at $z=0,1$, together with
$DW=0$, $Z=0$ on rigid surfaces or $D^{2}W=0$, $DZ=0$ on free
surfaces.  The boundary conditions themselves are local (they do not
involve time derivatives) and hence are insensitive to the
BDNK causal modifications.

The \emph{algorithm} for deciding whether stationary or oscillatory
onset occurs remains unchanged:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Compute $\Rarel^{(c)}$ -- the critical Rayleigh number for
    stationary convection (setting $\sigma=0$).
  \item Compute $\Rarel^{(o)}$ -- the critical Rayleigh number for
    overstability (requiring $\Rarel$ to be real with
    $\sigma = i\omega$, $\omega\ne 0$).
  \item The smaller of $\Rarel^{(c)}$ and $\Rarel^{(o)}$ is the
    physical onset value.
\end{enumerate}

The justification proceeds exactly as in \S\,29\,$(a)$: the
cubic~\eqref{eq:rel-3-cubic} at $\Rarel = 0$ has all roots with
$\mathrm{Re}(\sigma)<0$ (absolute stability), and as $\Rarel$
increases, either a real root passes through zero (stationary onset,
$D=0$ in the notation of~(241)) or the real part of a
complex-conjugate pair passes through zero (overstable onset,
$BC-D=0$).

In the BDNK first-order formalism, no additional relaxation-mode
roots appear; the characteristic equation has the same number of
roots as the classical one.

\section{Overstability solutions for various boundary conditions
  (\S31 -- relativistic)}\label{sec:rel-3-31}

\subsection{Method of solution}

The variational method of \S\,30 applies without modification to the
relativistic problem: one expands $F_{\mathrm{rel}}$ in the cosine
series~(260), obtains the secular determinant~(270) with the
replacements
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rel-3-redef-q}
  q_{j}^{2} \;\text{roots of}\;\;
  \left(q^{2}-a^{2}-\frac{i\sigma}{1+\tauq\,i\sigma}\right)^{2}
  (q^{2}-a^{2})
  + \mathscr{T}_{\mathrm{rel}}\,q^{2} = 0,
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rel-3-redef-chi}
  \chi_{j} = q_{j}^{2} - a^{2} - \frac{i\sigma}{1+\tauq\,i\sigma},
  \qquad
  c_{2m+1} = (2m+1)^{2}\pi^{2} + a^{2}
    + \frac{i\sigma}{1+\tauq\,i\sigma},
\end{equation}
and all remaining definitions ($\gamma_{2m+1}$, $B_{j}^{(m)}$,
$\langle m|n\rangle$) inheriting the new values of $q_{j}$, $\chi_{j}$,
$c_{2m+1}$.

In the first approximation (retaining only the $(0,0)$ element), the
characteristic equation~(271) becomes
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rel-3-R-approx}
  \Rarel
  = \frac{1}{a^{2}}\,
    \frac{\pi^{2}+a^{2}+i\hat{p}\sigma}
         {c_{1}\,\gamma_{1}\,\langle 0|0\rangle},
\end{equation}
with $\hat{p}\sigma$ as in~\eqref{eq:rel-3-hatsigma-def}.

\subsection{Numerical results: two free boundaries}

For $p = 0.025$ (mercury) and with BDNK frame coefficients
appropriate for liquid metals at laboratory conditions, the
relativistic corrections to the
critical Rayleigh number for overstability are:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rel-3-Rc-free-free}
  R_{c,\mathrm{rel}}^{(o)}
  = R_{c}^{(o)}\!\left[
    1 + \xi_{\mathrm{rel}}\,\frac{p_{0}}{\rdensity c^{2}}
    + \xi_{\mathrm{fr}}\,\frac{v^{2}}{c^{2}}
  \right]
  + \order{\left(\frac{p_{0}}{\rdensity c^{2}}\right)^{2}},
\end{equation}
where $R_{c}^{(o)}$ is the classical value from Table~XI and the
dimensionless coefficients $\xi_{\mathrm{rel}}$, $\xi_{\mathrm{fr}}$ are
functions of $x_{\min}$, $p$, and $T_{1}$.

In the large-rotation limit $p^{2}T\to\infty$, the asymptotic
formulae~(234)--(236) generalise to
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rel-3-Rc-asymp}
  R_{c,\mathrm{rel}}^{(o)}
  \;\to\;
  \frac{6\,p^{4/3}}{(1+p)^{1/3}}\,
  (\tfrac{1}{2}\pi^{2}T_{\mathrm{rel}})^{2/3}
  \relcorr{\frac{p_{0}}{\rdensity c^{2}}},
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rel-3-ac-asymp}
  a_{c,\mathrm{rel}}^{(o)}
  \;\to\;
  \left(\frac{p}{1+p}\right)^{1/3}
  (\tfrac{1}{2}\pi^{2}T_{\mathrm{rel}})^{1/6}
  \relcorr{\frac{p_{0}}{\rdensity c^{2}}},
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rel-3-sigma-asymp}
  |\sigma|_{\mathrm{rel}}
  \;\to\;
  \frac{(2-3p)^{1/2}}{|p(1+p)|^{1/2}}\,
  (\tfrac{1}{2}\pi^{2}T_{\mathrm{rel}})^{1/2}
  \relcorr{\frac{\tauq\,\nu}{d^{2}}}.
\end{equation}
The important feature is that the $T^{2/3}$ and $T^{1/2}$ scaling
laws are \emph{unchanged} by the relativistic corrections; only the
prefactors receive $\order{p_0/(\rdensity c^{2})}$ modifications.

\subsection{Numerical results: two rigid boundaries}

For two rigid boundaries with $p = 0.025$, the relativistic
corrections to Table~XII values follow the same structure
as~\eqref{eq:rel-3-Rc-free-free}:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rel-3-Rc-rigid}
  R_{c,\mathrm{rel}}^{(o)}\big|_{\text{rigid--rigid}}
  = R_{c}^{(o)}\big|_{\text{rigid--rigid}}
    \relcorr{\frac{p_{0}}{\rdensity c^{2}}}.
\end{equation}
The correction coefficients differ from the free-boundary case because
the eigenfunction structure (involving $\cosh q_{j}z$ factors) depends
on the roots~\eqref{eq:rel-3-redef-q}, which themselves carry the
BDNK modification.

\subsection{Numerical results: one free, one rigid boundary}

Similarly, for the mixed boundary conditions (Table~XIII),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rel-3-Rc-mixed}
  R_{c,\mathrm{rel}}^{(o)}\big|_{\text{rigid--free}}
  = R_{c}^{(o)}\big|_{\text{rigid--free}}
    \relcorr{\frac{p_{0}}{\rdensity c^{2}}}.
\end{equation}

\subsection*{Causality verification}

\begin{causalitycheck}
The BDNK formulation guarantees that no thermal signal
propagates faster than the characteristic speed
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rel-3-vth}
  v_{\mathrm{th}}
  = \sqrt{\frac{\thermcond}{\tauq\,c_{v}\,\rdensity}}
  \;\le\; c,
\end{equation}
where the inequality is ensured by the thermodynamic stability
condition $\tauq \ge \thermcond/(\rdensity c_{v} c^{2})$.  This
bound applies throughout the overstability analysis:
\begin{itemize}
  \item The oscillation frequency $\omega$ of the marginal overstable
    mode satisfies $\omega \ll 1/\tauq$ in the hydrodynamic regime,
    so the effective thermal propagation speed
    $v_{\mathrm{eff}} \sim \omega/k \sim (\kappa_{T}\omega)^{1/2}
    \ll v_{\mathrm{th}} \le c$.
  \item In the BDNK first-order formalism, no spurious relaxation
    modes appear; the dissipative signal speeds are bounded by~$c$
    through the coupled inequalities on the transport and frame
    coefficients.
  \item The cubic dispersion relation~\eqref{eq:rel-3-cubic}
    yields only physical hydrodynamic modes, all with phase velocities
    $|\omega/k| \le c$ for real~$k$.
\end{itemize}
In contrast, the classical (Fourier) heat equation admits infinite
propagation speed; the BDNK theory resolves this pathology.
The overstability results of \S\S\,29--31 are therefore
\emph{fully causal} in the relativistic framework.
\end{causalitycheck}

\section{Astrophysical application: overstability in neutron star
cores---BDNK cubic vs.\ classical}\label{sec:rel-3-app-29-31}
% ====================================================================

The overstability analysis of \S\S\,\ref{sec:rel-3-29}--\ref{sec:rel-3-31}
has direct consequences for oscillation modes in neutron star cores,
where the extremely low Prandtl number ($\mathrm{Pr} \sim 10^{-3}$)
and enormous Taylor number ($\Tarel \sim 10^{12}$--$10^{24}$)
place the system firmly in the overstable regime.

\subsection{Growth rates: BDNK vs.\ classical vs.\ Israel--Stewart}

The key advantage of the BDNK first-order causal formulation over
the Israel--Stewart (IS) second-order theory is the preservation
of the cubic character of the dispersion relation.  For neutron star
core matter, we can estimate the relevant relaxation timescales:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Thermal relaxation time:
    $\tauq \sim \ell_{\mathrm{mfp}}^{e}/c
    \approx 10^{-2}~\mathrm{cm} / (3 \times 10^{10}~\mathrm{cm/s})
    \sim 3 \times 10^{-13}$~s,
    where $\ell_{\mathrm{mfp}}^{e}$ is the electron mean free path;
  \item Viscous relaxation time:
    $\taupi \sim \ell_{\mathrm{mfp}}^{n}/v_{F}^{n}
    \approx 10^{-12}$~s,
    where $v_{F}^{n}$ is the neutron Fermi velocity.
\end{itemize}

The overstable oscillation frequency at $\mathrm{Pr} \to 0$ is given
by equation~\eqref{eq:rel-3-280}:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rel-3-app-fosc}
  f_{\mathrm{osc}}
  = \frac{1}{2\pi}\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^{1/2}2\Omega
    \left(\frac{\rdensity c^{2}}{\enthalpy}\right)^{1/2}.
\end{equation}
For a 500~Hz pulsar with $\enthalpy/(\rdensity c^{2}) = 1.3$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rel-3-app-fosc-num}
  f_{\mathrm{osc}}
  \approx 0.816 \times 2 \times 3142
    \times (1/1.3)^{1/2}
  \approx 4490~\mathrm{Hz}.
\end{equation}
The relativistic enthalpy correction reduces this by $\sim 12\%$
compared to the classical prediction of $\sim 5130$~Hz.

The dimensionless product $\tauq \omega$ that controls the
departure of the BDNK cubic from the classical cubic is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rel-3-app-tauomega}
  \tauq \cdot 2\pi f_{\mathrm{osc}}
  \sim 3 \times 10^{-13} \times 2.8 \times 10^{4}
  \sim 10^{-8},
\end{equation}
which is extremely small.  This confirms that for neutron star
core modes, the BDNK corrections to the classical cubic are
perturbatively small at $\mathcal{O}(\tauq^{2}\omega^{2}) \sim 10^{-16}$.

However, the BDNK formulation remains essential for two reasons:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item It guarantees that no spurious relaxation modes appear at
    frequencies $\sim 1/\tauq \sim 10^{12}$~Hz, which would be
    unphysical artefacts of the IS formulation;
  \item It ensures that the thermal signal speed remains bounded
    by $c$, which is critical for the stability of numerical
    simulations of neutron star convection (Radice et al.\ 2017).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Comparison of growth rates near onset}

Near the marginal state $\mathrm{Ra} = \mathrm{Ra}_{c}^{(o)}$,
the growth rate of the overstable mode scales as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rel-3-app-gamma}
  \gamma
  \approx \frac{\mathrm{Ra} - \mathrm{Ra}_{c}^{(o)}}{\mathrm{Ra}_{c}^{(o)}}
  \times (\pi^{2} + a^{2}) \times \mathrm{Pr}
  \times \frac{\rdensity c^{2}}{\enthalpy},
\end{equation}
where the last factor is the relativistic correction.  For
$\enthalpy/(\rdensity c^{2}) = 1.3$, this represents a 23\% reduction
in the growth rate compared to the classical estimate, leading to
a longer time to develop fully nonlinear convection.

For nuclear matter with $\mathrm{Pr} = 10^{-3}$ and
$\mathrm{Ra}/\mathrm{Ra}_{c}^{(o)} = 2$, the dimensional growth
timescale is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rel-3-app-tgrowth}
  t_{\mathrm{grow}}
  = \frac{d^{2}}{\nu \cdot \gamma}
  \approx \frac{(10^{4})^{2}}{10 \times 10}
  \sim 10^{6}~\mathrm{s}
  \approx 12~\mathrm{days}.
\end{equation}

\section{BDNK cubic versus Israel--Stewart quintic: mode structure in
neutron star cores}\label{sec:rel-3-deep-bdnk-is}

We now provide a detailed comparison of the BDNK and Israel--Stewart
dispersion relations for overstability in neutron star cores, explicitly
computing the mode frequencies and demonstrating that the two extra
IS modes are unphysical artefacts.

\subsection{Setup: NS core oscillation modes}

Consider a 500~Hz pulsar with $\xi = 1.28$ (SLy EOS), giving
$T_{1,\rm rel} = \Tarel/\pi^4 \approx 6.6 \times 10^{19}$,
$\mathrm{Pr} = 7.8 \times 10^{-5}$, and relaxation times
$\tau_q \approx 3\times 10^{-13}$~s,
$\tau_\pi \approx 10^{-12}$~s.

The non-dimensional time unit is $d^2/\nu_{\rm eff} \approx 1.3 \times 10^7$~s,
so the non-dimensional relaxation times are:
\begin{equation}
  \hat\tau_q = \frac{\tau_q}{d^2/\nu_{\rm eff}} \approx 2.3 \times 10^{-20},
  \qquad
  \hat\tau_\pi \approx 7.8 \times 10^{-20}.
\end{equation}
These are extraordinarily small, confirming that relaxation effects
are negligible on hydrodynamic timescales.

\subsection{The BDNK cubic}

The BDNK dispersion relation for two free boundaries
(equation~\eqref{eq:rel-3-cubic}) is a cubic in $\sigma$:
\begin{equation}
  p\,s\,\sigma^3
  + s^2(1 + 2p)\,\sigma^2
  + \bigl[s^3(2+p) + p\,T_{1,\rm rel}\,\pi^4 - R_1\,x\,\pi^4\bigr]\,\sigma
  + s^4 + s\,T_{1,\rm rel}\,\pi^4 - R_1\,x\,s\,\pi^4
  = 0,
\end{equation}
where $s = 1 + x$, $x = a^2/\pi^2$, $R_1 = \Rarel/\pi^4$,
$p = \mathrm{Pr}$.  This has exactly \textbf{three roots},
corresponding to:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item One thermal mode (primarily driven by the heat equation);
  \item Two vorticity/inertial modes (coupled by Coriolis force).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{The Israel--Stewart quintic}

In the IS formulation, the replacements
$\sigma \to \sigma/(1 + \hat\tau_q\sigma)$ in the thermal sector and
$p\sigma \to p\sigma/(1 + \hat\tau_\pi\sigma)$ in the viscous sector
transform the cubic into a \textbf{quintic}:
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{P}_5(\sigma) = (1 + \hat\tau_q\sigma)^2(1 + \hat\tau_\pi\sigma)\,
  \mathcal{P}_3^{\rm IS}(\sigma) = 0.
\end{equation}
The five roots consist of:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Three physical hydrodynamic modes (continuous deformations of the
    BDNK cubic roots);
  \item One thermal relaxation mode at $\sigma \approx -1/\hat\tau_q
    \approx -4.3 \times 10^{19}$;
  \item One viscous relaxation mode at $\sigma \approx -1/\hat\tau_\pi
    \approx -1.3 \times 10^{19}$.
\end{enumerate}

The two extra modes decay on the relaxation timescale
$\sim \hat\tau_{q,\pi} \cdot d^2/\nu_{\rm eff}
\sim 10^{-20} \times 10^7~\text{s} \sim 10^{-13}$~s.  In physical
terms, these modes correspond to the relaxation of the heat flux and
shear stress toward their Navier--Stokes values.  They have:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Growth rates $|\mathrm{Re}(\sigma)| \sim 10^{19}$ (in
    non-dimensional units), i.e.\ extremely rapid decay;
  \item Oscillation frequencies $|\mathrm{Im}(\sigma)| \sim 1/\hat\tau_{q,\pi}
    \sim 10^{19}$--$10^{20}$, corresponding to physical frequencies
    $\sim 10^{12}$~Hz.
\end{itemize}

These modes are \emph{unphysical} in the following precise sense:
(i)~their frequencies exceed the inverse mean free path
frequency $c/\ell_{\rm mfp}$, violating the hydrodynamic
approximation; (ii)~they are absent in the BDNK formulation, which
achieves causality without introducing spurious modes; (iii)~they
can cause numerical instabilities in simulations unless treated
with implicit timestepping.

\subsection{Comparison of physical mode eigenvalues}

The three physical modes of the IS quintic differ from the BDNK cubic
roots by corrections of order $\hat\tau_q^2 \omega^2 \sim 10^{-16}$:
\begin{equation}
  \sigma_n^{\rm IS} = \sigma_n^{\rm BDNK}
  \bigl[1 + \order{\hat\tau_q^2 \omega^2}\bigr],
  \qquad n = 1, 2, 3.
\end{equation}
This confirms that for \emph{all} physically relevant neutron star
core conditions, the BDNK and IS formulations give identical
predictions for the onset of overstability.  The advantage of BDNK is
the \emph{absence} of spurious modes, which simplifies both analytical
and numerical treatment.

\begin{relcorrection}
The BDNK first-order causal framework preserves the cubic character of
the overstability dispersion relation, yielding exactly three physical
modes.  The Israel--Stewart second-order formulation produces a quintic
with two additional relaxation modes that decay on timescales
$\sim\tau_{q,\pi} \sim 10^{-13}$~s---eight orders of magnitude faster
than the hydrodynamic oscillation period.  These modes are unphysical
artefacts of the relaxation-time approximation and do not affect the
onset or character of overstable convection.  The BDNK formulation is
therefore preferred for stability analysis in neutron star cores.
\end{relcorrection}

of the BDNK cubic and IS quintic as functions of Rayleigh number.
